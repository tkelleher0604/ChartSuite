# QA as the General Counsel

A pass through Rboreal as the fund's general counsel getting ready for a board meeting and answering partners' questions, and as the person presenting the app to the team. Tested on production at `34eb7c5`, with the sample chart on 2026-10-25, in headless Chrome at 1440×900, 1920×1080 and 1280×720.

**How it was driven.** Each page was copied beside itself with two scripts injected: one in `<head>` that turns errors into a red banner and catches downloads, and one after load that clicks, types and reads the page. Exports came through `exportOptions.onFile` and the page's download link, as base64 in the DOM, and were then read with `unzip` and `pdftotext`. No JavaScript error or red banner appeared in any flow.

**Screenshots and scripts** were in the QA session's scratch folder, `gcqa\` (temporary, and not in the repository); the paths below are within it. The harness is `probe.sh` there (`probe.sh <page> <hash> <png|.dom> <steps.js> [W] [H] [budget] [head.js] [prefix-js]`), and each step script is in `steps\`.

## What impressed me

- **Present mode.** Full screen, fitted, the arrow keys stepping through the four views with a caption, and the red required-entry marks hidden. It's the best way to show the chart.
- **Clicking a purple line explains it.** On the 40 Act view, one click on Moretti's dashed line opens the panel with the §57(b)(2) chain. That is the "why" a lawyer wants, right on the chart.
- **"Who can sign for the AIV?"** is answered at a glance on the record page: Chen and Okafor, via GP III. The Fund III profile adds Moretti's $5,000,000 limit.
- **History tells Corvane's story** crisply: who, when, 56% → 49%, a record filter kept in the address, and Restore that says plainly when it can't.
- **The Export dialog** previews every page or slide live. The PowerPoint is native, editable shapes (88 shapes on the Fund III slide), and "every view" makes a 5-slide deck. The PDF is vector, with real text.
- **The SharePoint conflict** is clear and humane. The card says who saved and when, all three ways out work, the sidebar shows "Not saved: Margaret A. Chen saved a newer version", and an edit made on another page raises the same notice.
- **Home and the Calendar** agree, and give a crisp answer to "what's overdue and what's next", with Why on every computed date.
- **Documents** reads like a real DMS index: the preview pane shows the library's own metadata and versions, and Not linked lists files no record points to.
- **Export and import of the workbook** round-trip cleanly, with a clear Replace or Add choice that Undo takes back.
- No errors anywhere, in light or dark.

## The three things that would most strengthen the demo

1. **Make the exported slide board-ready.** Keep chart text at 8 pt or more by laying views out to suit the page, not by shrinking. Make the title slide optional. Use board wording in the legend ("Not in good standing", no "Kind"). Fix or explain the Calder Ridge III Blocker's good standing, so no unexplained red box goes on the board slide. This is the deliverable in the headline question (findings 1, 2, 12, 15).
2. **Make "what's missing" a first-class answer.** List missing governing documents and not-in-good-standing entities on Home and in the record header. Flag required entries at relations, such as Raman's start date. Make each Why say what's missing, or what was never recorded, rather than echoing the formula. This is the question a GC asks before every board meeting (findings 3, 4, 6, 14).
3. **Set the stage for presenting.** Open the chart fitted and readable with the panel collapsed. Ask for the presenter's name before the first change, so the History doesn't say "You". Give the palette aliases and global actions ("Fund III", "overdue", "Present the chart"). Add a short guided tour or a demo script with real placeholder documents to open (findings 5, 8, 9, 16).
