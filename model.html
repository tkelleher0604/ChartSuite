The Rboreal Model

The whole of what the prototype stores and computes, built up from the trees of data-model.md: forms, formulas, names, styles, views, charts and workbooks.

data-model.md defines records as labeled trees and states as sets of them. This document carries that theory up through everything else the app keeps. It describes the app as built: each definition here has a counterpart in UI/mockups/app, and the figures are worked out, live, by the app's own code from the sample chart. Where writing the model down showed part of the app to be unprincipled, the app was changed to fit; §9 lists the changes. Formulas are only summarized here; formulas.md has them in full.

At a glance

A workbook is a nonempty sequence of charts, which share nothing. A chart holds a schema, the records entered in it, the definitions whose formulas compute more records, and several lesser structures; its views, of which there is at least one, each draw the same records their own way.

$$\begin{aligned} \mathsf{Workbook} &= \mathsf{Chart}^{+}, & \mathsf{Chart} &= (S,\ E,\ F,\ R,\ N,\ O,\ A,\ V),\\ V &\in \mathsf{View}^{+}, & \mathsf{View} &= (Y,\ \lambda,\ H,\ \varphi). \end{aligned}$$

1Trees, recalled

We use the terms of data-model.md throughout: kinds \(\mathcal K\) and their objects, datatypes \(\mathcal D\) and their values, linkages, specifications, fields, tags, choices, locations, parameters, relations, schemas, records, arguments, argumentations, outputs, parameterizations, dependencies and states. For a specification or record \(t\) we write \(\operatorname{out}(t)\) for its output, \(\operatorname{par}(t)\) for its parameterization (for a record, the parameterization of its specification), and \(\operatorname{Loc}(t)\) for its set of locations. For a record \(r\), \(\operatorname{spec}(r)\) is its underlying specification. For a schema \(S\), \(\operatorname{Par}(S)\) is the set of parameterizations of its specifications.

1.1Labels. Text labels (kinds, edge labels, options) are compared without regard to case: trees that differ only in the case of text labels are the same tree. A label is displayed as it was first written.

1.2Datatypes. \(\mathcal D = \{\mathsf{text}, \mathsf{number}, \mathsf{percent}, \mathsf{money}, \mathsf{date}\}\), with \(T(\mathsf{text})\) the strings, \(T(\mathsf{number}) = T(\mathsf{percent}) = T(\mathsf{money}) = \mathbb R\), and \(T(\mathsf{date})\) the calendar dates. Datatypes with the same values differ only in how values are written and read: \(\mathsf{percent}\) writes \(0.716\) as 71.6%, and \(\mathsf{money}\) writes \(7.5 \times 10^{8}\) as $750,000,000.

1.3Identity. A state may contain distinct records with the same tree only if they are objects, which are told apart by identity alone. The app gives every record an id, and a record's tree refers to its locations by their ids.

2Forms

A parameterization says exactly which kinds an entry relates, and under which options. Much of what the app does wants something coarser: one style for every ownership whatever kind the owner is, one name for Tax Classification under every Entity Type. That coarser thing is the form.

2.1The shape of a tree \(t\), \(\operatorname{sh}(t)\), is the tree obtained from \(t\) by removing the labels of all of its nodes. Its edges keep their labels.

2.2The sort of a specification \(s\) is \(\operatorname{so}(s) = d\) if \(s\) is a field with datatype \(d\); \(\mathsf{choice}\) or \(\mathsf{tag}\) if it is a choice or tag; and \(\mathsf{object}\) if it is a lone kind, whose records are objects.

2.3The form of \(s\) is \(\operatorname{fm}(s) = (\operatorname{sh}(s), \operatorname{so}(s))\).

2.4In a schema, specifications with the same parameterization have the same form. They have the same shape, since a shape doesn't see the root's label; and by the schema conditions they are all fields, which then coincide, or all tags, or all choices, so they have the same sort. We may therefore speak of the form \(\operatorname{fm}(p)\) of a parameterization \(p \in \operatorname{Par}(S)\), and of the parameterizations \(\operatorname{Par}_S(f)\) and outputs \(\operatorname{Out}_S(f) = \{\operatorname{out}(s) : s \in S,\ \operatorname{fm}(s) = f\}\) of a form \(f\).

2.5Forms partition \(\operatorname{Par}(S)\) more coarsely than identity. A form forgets two things: which kinds sit at the leaves, and which options or attestations lie on the paths between the root and the leaves. So the three parameterizations of Ownership (an Entity, an Individual or an Investor Group as owner) share a form; so do Tax Classification under each of three Entity Types, and Name on every kind. A form keeps its edge labels, its sort, and how deep each location sits: an entry located at an object and one located at a choice under that object have different forms.

2.6The keying principle. What a record means is keyed by its parameterization; what it looks like and what it is called, by its form; where it sits, by its location. Accordingly, formulas and required entries attach to parameterizations (§3–4), styles and names to forms (§3, §5), and entry order and box positions to locations (§3, §5).

3Charts

3.1A chart is a tuple \(C = (S, E, F, R, N, O, A, V)\) of

  • a schema \(S\), held explicitly (3.2);
  • a finite set \(E\) of records in \(S\), the entered records;
  • a finite set \(F\) of definitions (§4), from which with \(E\) comes the chart's computed state \(\Sigma = \Sigma(E, F)\), a state in \(S\);
  • a set \(R \subseteq \operatorname{Par}(S)\) of required parameterizations (3.4);
  • names \(N\) for relation forms and their lists (3.5);
  • an order \(O\) on entries (3.6);
  • an archive \(A\) of deleted records (3.7);
  • and a nonempty sequence \(V\) of views (§5).

\(E\) need not be a state by itself, since an entered record may be located at a computed one; it is \(\Sigma\) that must be.

3.2The schema grows. Entering a record adds its specification to \(S\). Nothing removes a specification except removing it deliberately, which is allowed only for one that is unused: no record of \(\Sigma\) has it, no definition's scope or \(R\) includes its parameterization, and nothing in \(O\) is keyed by it. So \(S\) is the vocabulary the chart has used, not merely what its records use now, and a choice keeps its options when no record has them. An unused specification also gives way where keeping it would break the schema conditions: when a definition changes its sort, or a record is entered whose specification conflicts with it.

3.3Because \(S\) is explicit, every question about the schema (what entries a location may have, which options a choice offers, which relations two objects may enter, what a style or formula is about) is answered from \(S\), not by looking for a record that happens to have the answer.

3.4Required entries. For \(p \in R\) whose single parameter has location specification \(\ell\), a record \(x \in \Sigma\) with \(\operatorname{spec}(x) = \ell\) lacks \(p\) if no record of \(\Sigma\) located at \(x\) has parameterization \(p\). A tag counts as present whether attested or ruled out. Lacked entries are drawn as blanks to fill in; leaving an object that lacks one asks first.

3.5Names. A relation form is a form whose root has more than one child. \(N\) assigns to some relation forms \(f\) a name together with an order on \(f\)'s root edge labels, the order in which a call to the relation takes its arguments. It also assigns to some triples \((f, e, e')\), with \(e \ne e'\) root edge labels of \(f\), a list name: the name, seen from an argument in role \(e\), of its relatives in role \(e'\). A list without a name goes by \(e'\); one with a name answers to \(e'\) as well.

3.6Order. \(O\) assigns to some location specifications \(\ell\) a finite sequence of labels. The entries of a record with specification \(\ell\) are listed with Name first, then those whose labels occur in \(O(\ell)\), in that order, then the rest by type (fields, tags, choices) and alphabetically.

3.7Archive. \(A\) is a sequence of batches, each a label, a time, and a set of entered records deleted together with their dependents (7.2).

4Formulas

Formulas are set out in full in formulas.md; this section restates what the rest of the model needs.

4.1A definition is a pair \(\phi = (P_\phi, \rho_\phi)\) of a nonempty scope \(P_\phi \subseteq \operatorname{Par}(S)\), all of one form, and a rule \(\rho_\phi\): a value formula, or, if the scope's sort is \(\mathsf{tag}\), a nonempty finite sequence of labeled implications. The scopes of distinct definitions are disjoint. A definition may be marked reflexive, letting one object fill two of its parameters.

4.2A definition's name is its form's name in \(N\) if that is a relation form, and otherwise the label of its form's only root edge. Its sort, datatype and options are read from \(S\). The definition stores none of these: it is only a scope and a rule. Its rule is written in the formula language of formulas.md.

4.3For a parameterization \(p\) and a set of records \(\Sigma\), a candidate for \(p\) in \(\Sigma\) is an argumentation obtained from \(p\) by replacing each of its locations with a record of \(\Sigma\) whose specification is that location, using no object twice unless the definition is reflexive. At a candidate, a value formula gives an output or nothing; an implication holds or doesn't.

4.4The computed state. \(\Sigma(E, F)\) is the set of records \(r\) such that either \(r \in E\), or \(r\)'s argumentation \(a\) is a candidate in \(\Sigma\) for a parameterization in the scope of some \(\phi \in F\), no record of \(E\) has argumentation \(a\), and either \(\phi\) gives \(\operatorname{out}(r)\) at \(a\) or \(\operatorname{out}(r) = \mathsf{True}\) and one of \(\phi\)'s implications holds at \(a\), reading \(\Sigma\) throughout. The records of \(\Sigma \setminus E\) are computed.

$$\Sigma \;=\; E \;\cup\; \bigl\{\, (a, o) \;:\; a \in \operatorname{Cand}_\Sigma(P_\phi),\ \phi \in F,\ a \notin \operatorname{arg}(E),\ \phi \text{ gives } o \text{ at } a \text{ reading } \Sigma \,\bigr\}$$

\(\Sigma\) appears on both sides. Records are worked out in the order they depend on each other; where some depend on themselves through implications alone, and monotonely, \(\Sigma\) is the least solution; any other record that depends on itself is left out and marked circular (formulas.md, "Loops").

4.5Entered records win. Where \(E\) has a record with argumentation \(a\), no formula's output at \(a\) enters \(\Sigma\). An entered ruling-out of a tag that an implication holds for is how a presumption is rebutted.

4.6Identity of computed records. A computed record's id is made from its argumentation, so it is the same each time \(\Sigma\) is worked out, and records entered at it (a note on a computed Control, say) stay put. Changing a computed record enters it under the same id, as an override.

5Views

5.1A view is a tuple \(v = (Y_v, \lambda_v, H_v, \varphi_v)\) of a sequence of styles, a layout, a set of hidden records, and an optional filter. Every view of a chart draws the same computed state \(\Sigma\).

5.2A formatting is a partial function from the properties \(\mathcal P = \mathcal P_{\mathrm{box}} \cup \mathcal P_{\mathrm{text}} \cup \{\mathsf{show}, \mathsf{format}\}\) to values, where \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{box}}\) = {shape, fill, outline, outline weight, dash} and \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{text}}\) = {font, size, bold, italic, color}. A line uses the outline properties of \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{box}}\).

5.3A style is a pair \(y = (f_y, m_y)\) of a form and a partial function \(m_y : \operatorname{Out}_S(f_y) \rightharpoonup \mathrm{Fmt}\) from its outputs to formattings. \(Y_v\) is a finite sequence of styles on distinct forms. The style on the form of a lone kind is the Kind style; its outputs are the kinds.

5.4Effective formatting. For \(r \in \Sigma\), let \(y\) be the style of \(Y_v\) on \(\operatorname{fm}(\operatorname{spec}(r))\), if there is one. Then

$$p_v(r) \;=\; D(r) \;\oplus\; m_y\bigl(\operatorname{out}(r)\bigr),$$

where \(\oplus\) is union favoring its right side and \(D(r)\) is the default: \(\mathsf{show} = \mathsf{True}\) for an object, \(\mathsf{False}\) for an entry, and for a relation \(\mathsf{True}\) unless a definition's scope includes a parameterization of its form, or \(r\) is a ruled-out tag. So \(\mathsf{show}\) says whether a record is drawn, whatever the record: an object as a box, an entry as a line of text, a relation as a line.

5.5What a view draws. Write \(\mathsf{show}_v(r)\) for \(p_v(r)(\mathsf{show})\). The view \(v\) draws

  • the box of an object \(x\) iff \(\mathsf{show}_v(x)\), \(x \notin H_v\), and \(\varphi_v\) is absent or holds at \(x\);
  • the line of a two-argument relation \(r\) iff \(\mathsf{show}_v(r)\), \(r \notin H_v\), and \(v\) draws the boxes of both its arguments' objects;
  • an entry \(e\) under an object \(x\) as a line of text in \(x\)'s box iff \(\mathsf{show}_v(e)\) and \(v\) draws the box;
  • an entry \(e\) under a relation \(r\) as part of \(r\)'s label iff \(\mathsf{show}_v(e)\) and \(v\) draws the line (an ownership's Percentage).

5.6How a box looks. Let \(\Sigma_x\) be the records under the object \(x\): \(x\) and its entries, depth first, in the order \(O\) lists them. The box properties of \(x\)'s box are

$$\operatorname{box}_v(x) \;=\; B_0 \;\oplus \bigoplus_{y \in Y_v}\; \bigoplus_{\substack{r \in \Sigma_x\\ \operatorname{fm}(r) = f_y}} m_y\bigl(\operatorname{out}(r)\bigr)\big|_{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{box}}},$$

folding left to right, so a later style overrides an earlier one, and within a style a later record an earlier one. Text properties fold the same way over the records not drawn as lines of text, applying to the whole box; a record drawn as a line of text adds its own text properties to its line. Order in \(Y_v\) matters only where two styles set one property of one box, as the Kind style's fill and Vehicle Type's fill do.

5.7Layout. \(\lambda_v\) maps some objects to a position and width. Objects it doesn't place are placed automatically, below the rest.

5.8Filter. \(\varphi_v\) is an expression in the formula language, worked out at each object as an entry's formula at that object would be. It holds where its value is TRUE. A filter that runs into an error at an object leaves its box drawn, marked.

6Workbooks

6.1A workbook is a nonempty sequence of charts \(W = (C_1, \dots, C_n)\), together with a sequence of deleted charts that can be restored. Charts share nothing: no records, schema, formulas, names or views.

6.2Copying formulas. Definitions move between charts only by copying. Copying from \(C'\) into \(C\) replaces \(C\)'s formulas and names by those of \(C'\) where their names agree, and adds the rest; their specifications join \(S\). The charts stay unsynced: a later change to either doesn't reach the other.

6.3Which chart and which view are current, and each chart's undo history, are not part of the model. They belong to the session.

7Edits

Every edit is a transition \(C \to C'\), taken as one undoable step; \(\Sigma\) is worked out again from \(E'\) and \(F'\). Undo puts back the chart as it was, \(S\) included.

7.1Entering. An edit to records replaces \(E\) by \(E'\), and \(S' = S \cup \{\operatorname{spec}(r) : r \in E'\}\). Changing a computed record enters it, under its id, as an override (4.6). The result must be a state in a schema: otherwise the edit is refused, with the reason.

7.2Deleting. Deleting records \(X\) removes them with their dependents: \(E' = E \setminus \operatorname{dep}(X)\), where \(\operatorname{dep}(X)\) is the least set containing \(X\) and every record located at a member of it. The entered records removed join \(A\) as a batch; restoring the batch puts them back, once what they depend on is back.

7.3Key maps. An edit that changes specifications is described by a map \(\mu : S \to S'\). Because parameterizations and forms are functions of specifications, \(\mu\) induces maps on them, and these carry everything keyed by them: \(O\) by location specification; \(R\) and definition scopes by parameterization; names and styles by form. Formula texts and filters that mention a renamed label are relabeled with it.

7.4Renaming every one like it. Renaming a label on a record \(r\) every one like it relabels that edge in every specification of \(S\) with \(r\)'s form, and in their dependents; \(\mu\) is that relabeling, and every record of those specifications is relabeled with it.

7.5The abandonment law. An edit to individual records (renaming here only, or moving an entry up a level) moves each edited record from a specification \(s\) to another, \(s'\). Then \(\mu(s) = s'\) for each such \(s\) that no record of \(E'\) has any more, and \(\mu\) is the identity elsewhere: a specification abandoned by an edit moves with the records that abandoned it. \(S' = \mu(S) \cup \{\operatorname{spec}(r) : r \in E'\}\).

7.6Definitions, names and views are edited directly: a definition's scope or rule, a name, a style's formatting, a box's position, the hidden set, the filter. Creating a definition adds to \(S\) the specifications its scope and sort require.

7.7Invariants. Every edit preserves the following, and an edit that would break one is refused. model-test.html checks them on the sample, and after each kind of edit.

  1. \(S\) is a schema: closed under locations, and satisfying data-model.md's conditions on shared parameterizations.
  2. Every record of \(\Sigma\) is a record in \(S\), and \(\Sigma\) is a state.
  3. Every definition's scope is a nonempty subset of \(\operatorname{Par}(S)\) within one form, and no two scopes meet.
  4. \(R \subseteq \operatorname{Par}(S)\), and \(N\) names only relation forms that \(S\) has.
  5. In each view, no two styles share a form, and each style's form is one that \(S\) has.

8Representation

The app stores each part of the model as plain data, and identifies trees by canonical strings.

8.1Keys. Let \(\nu\) fold a label's case. The key of a specification is

$$\kappa(s) = \begin{cases} \texttt{kind:}\,\nu(k) & \text{if } s \text{ is a lone kind } k,\\[2pt] \bigl[\,\operatorname{out}(s),\ \operatorname{sort}\bigl\{[\nu(e), \kappa(\ell)] : s \xrightarrow{\,e\,} \ell\bigr\}\,\bigr] & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}$$

written as JSON, the children sorted because the tree is unordered. A parameterization's key is the sorted list of its children's pairs without the output; a form's key is built the same way with \(\operatorname{so}(s)\) for the output and the shape of each location in place of its key, a kind becoming • and a linkage ∘.

8.2Records. An object is { kind }. An entry with one location is { at, edge, … } and one with several is { args: [{ edge, at }, …], … }, where at is a location's id, and the output is one of field and value, tag, or choice. The two shapes of entry are one notion, an entry with its arguments; the first is the second with a single argument, written shorter.

9Revisions

Writing the model down showed where the app had grown its own notions instead of the model's. Each of these was changed to fit the model above.

An explicit schema

The app kept no schema; it read one off its records, and where no record survived it kept scraps of schema elsewhere: required entries stored their label and type, styles cached their names and descriptions, formulas stored their type and datatype, and a choice lost its options with its last record. Now \(S\) is stored (3.2–3.3), and all of these read it.

Forms, and the keying principle

Styles were keyed by parameterization, so the sample needed three Name styles and six for ownership, and Show/Hide, renaming and relation names each grouped parameterizations their own way. Now one notion, the form (§2), serves all of them, and 2.6 says what is keyed by what.

One kind of value

Entered numbers were kept as the text typed ("71.6%") and computed ones as numbers, so the two compared unequal and formulas carried a flag to show theirs as percentages. Now values are values of their datatype's type (1.2), with percent and money as datatypes, and how a value is written follows from its datatype.

Definitions as scope and rule

A definition said what it covers in one of two ways (locations written as text, or parameterization keys) and carried a name, type and datatype that had to be kept in step with the names and the records. Now a definition is a scope and a rule (4.1–4.2); the rest is read from \(S\) and \(N\).

One meaning of show

A view could hide a record four ways: a style's show, the hidden set, the filter, and a separate list of hidden kinds. The last was a style in disguise: hiding a kind is now its Kind-style entry's \(\mathsf{show}\), and \(\mathsf{show}\) means one thing for every record (5.4).

Relations known by form throughout

Names were keyed by form, but the formula engine still told relations apart by their edge labels, so two forms with the same edges could share a name the model kept apart. The engine now knows a relation, and so its name and lists, by its form too.

Options are what a formula gives

A choice's options were taken from every quoted text in its formula, so Tax Classification offered "Corporation", which its formula only compares against. Now a formula contributes only the texts it can give: its value, or a branch of IF or IFS.

One place for the vocabulary

The app showed the chart's vocabulary in four places, each its own way: a list of formulas, a names editor, a schema list and the Show menus. Now the Schema view holds it all, form by form: each form's parameterizations and whether they are required, its outputs, its relation and list names, its definitions, and on the chart its style's \(\mathsf{show}\). The Show menu is one menu over forms. Each concept has one name throughout: a definition is a scope and a rule, its rule is written as a formula (or as implications), and what it gives is computed.

The abandonment law

What followed a record through a rename or a move was inferred after the fact, and only for keys some record still had. Now an edit describes its map on specifications (7.3), and 7.4–7.5 say exactly what moves.
