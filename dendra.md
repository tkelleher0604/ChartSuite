# Dendra

Dendra is a system of record for the legal team of a private fund manager, or any team that keeps track of entities. It keeps their entities, people and investor groups; the ownership, control, positions and signing authority between them; their registrations, deadlines and documents. It works out by formula what follows from these, draws structure charts from them, and exports the charts to PowerPoint and PDF.

This document is the design. Part 1 continues [data-model.md](data-model.md), whose terms it uses throughout, and builds the rest of the model from them in the same way. Part 2 is the window. Part 3 is the look and the words. Part 4 is what it's built with.

## Part 1. The model

### Time

A **period** is a finite union of spans of days, each "since a, before b", where either end may be absent.

A **dated record** is a record together with a nonempty period, its **validity**. A **timeline** in a schema S is a finite set of dated records of S in which no two distinct records with the same argumentation are valid on a common day, unless both are objects.

A record of a timeline **holds** on a day d if it is valid on d and each of its locations holds on d. The records that hold on d are **the chart as of d**. Sanity check: the chart as of d is a state. It is closed under locations by the definition of holding, and two of its records with one argumentation would both be valid on d.

A kind or relation may name a date entry at it as its **start** and another as its **end** (see Notes): an Entity's are Formation Date and Dissolution Date, a General Partner's Admission Date and Withdrawal Date. A record's validity is then its own period narrowed to the days from its start, if it has one, and before its end. So Formation Date stays an ordinary field, and a fund is in the chart as of a day only once it's formed.

So a record added holds from its start, whenever it's recorded: a fund added today is in the chart from its Formation Date, and an object with no start, a person, is in it all along. A relation with none, an ownership or a membership, has only its own period, so the day it begins is said as it's added: the day it began, or none, for what was always so.

A record deleted is the mirror. One whose kind or relation has an end entry ends by it, whenever that's recorded: deleting a fund gives it its Dissolution Date, a general partner its Withdrawal Date. Any other object or relation, an ownership say, ends on a day said as it's deleted: its period ends that day, with all that's at it, and it stays in the chart as of every day before. Or it never held, a mistake, and is deleted as if never made. So deleting today an owner who redeemed today leaves last year's chart as it was. The day a record **ends on**, by its period or its end entry, is the first day it no longer holds, everywhere it's said.

A relation of two sides that's entered and holds, not one a formula gives, can be **transferred**: from its first side, the holder its sentence begins with ("Fund III Blocker is an owner of …"), to another record that fits there, one the chart has or a new one; on a day; all of it, or, where it has an **amount**, the first figure entered at it in its entries' order (an ownership's Percentage), a part. A figure entered beside the amount that says the same (a Voting Percentage of the same 82%) is the amount said again. A transfer is one change, read in the chart as of its day, and writes:
- what it's from: all of it, the relation ends on the day, as deleting it ends it; a part, its amount, and each figure saying it again, less the part, from the day;
- what it's to: where the other record holds a relation of the same name and output with the same other side on the day, that one's amount, and each figure saying it again, more the part, from the day; or else a new relation of the same output, the other record at the first side and the other side as it was, holding from the day (its start entry given the day, where its relation has one), with the part as its amount and its figures saying it again, and a copy of every other entry entered at the old one but its dates, which were the old one's own; and a record new with it, of a kind its side takes, named, holding from the day.

History reads a transfer back from these records alone, as "Transferred 82% of Halvard Logistics Holdings, Inc. from Fund III Blocker to Northwind Holdings LLC on Nov 1, 2026", one without an amount by its option or name ("Transferred General Partner of Fund III from GP III to GP IV on …").

### Change

An **item** is anything in a workbook with an identity: a chart, a specification of its schema (so a kind or an option can exist before any record has it), a record, a note or a free shape. A **row** is a version of an item; a dated record's row carries its validity.

A **change** removes some rows and adds others, and says who made it and when. A **log** is a set of changes in which every row removed was added by a change in the log. A row is **live** if it was added and not removed. **A workbook is its log**: its timeline, notes, views and charts are its live rows.

The **reverse** of a change removes the rows it added and adds fresh copies of the rows it removed; it can be made while every row the change added is live. **Undo** reverses your latest change not yet reversed, and **Redo** your latest undo, if you have made no change since. An item is **deleted** when it has rows in the log and none live. **Restoring** it adds fresh copies of what the change that deleted it removed of it and of what was at it, and nothing else that change did; it can be made while what the item is at is live.

The **union** of two logs is a log, and taking unions is commutative, associative and idempotent. Saving anywhere is therefore: read the copy there, take the union, write it back. Live rows that break the timeline's condition **disagree**; only changes made without seeing each other can, and a person settles it by keeping one, which is a change.

The log as it stood at a moment keeps only the changes made by then. So a chart can be read two ways, independently: **as of a day** (the world's time) and **as it stood** at a moment (the time it was recorded).

### Notes

A **pattern** is a tree obtained from a record by replacing some of its objects with their kinds, its value (if it has one) with its datatype, and removing the labels of some of its nodes. A record **fits** each pattern obtained from it this way, and a pattern p is **narrower** than q if q can be obtained from p this way. A specification and a parameterization are patterns, and so is every record.

**View** is a kind every chart has, the app's own, never listed among the chart's kinds.

A **note** is an entry located at a pattern, and optionally also at a view, labeled by one of the app's **note labels**:

| Label | What it says of the records that fit |
| --- | --- |
| Name, Description | what a kind, entry or relation is called and means; on a parameter, the name of its list of relatives (Owners, Holdings) |
| Order | where an entry sits among its location's entries |
| Required | that a blank one needs attention |
| Check | that one ruled out needs attention: on a definition, that it checks something |
| Start, End | which entries are the start and end of what they're at |
| Formula | how its records are worked out (see Definitions) |
| Shown, Caption, Position, Text, and the format properties | how a view draws them (see Views): every label but the others here is one, so a new property is only a word and its drawing. Shape, Corner (a rounded rectangle's), Fill, Transparency (of the fill, 0–100), Outline, Weight, Dash, Shadow (none or soft), Frame, Layer, Arrow, Connector, Above; Font, Size, Bold, Italic, Underline, Strike, Color, Align, Valign (top, middle or bottom), Spacing (of lines, a multiple of single), Format |
| Layout, As Of, Page | on a View object: its layout, the day it keeps, its page (with its Name and Order, the view's own notes) |

**The narrowest decides.** For a record r and a label, of the notes with that label whose pattern r fits, the narrowest decides. In a view, every note at the view decides before any note at no view. Where notes reach one mark through different records, the view's order of its styles decides, the later winning.

Renaming relabels every tree in the chart, records, schema and the patterns of notes alike, so nothing needs re-keying. Moving an entry up a level is one substitution in every tree.

### Definitions

A Formula note is a **definition**. It is a list of **rows**, each a label and a formula, and it covers the records fitting its pattern. `← c` is short for `= IF(c, TRUE)`.

**Nothing.** BLANK is nothing: it drops out of a sum and wipes out a product. SUM, MIN and MAX of nothing are nothing, and COUNT is 0. A definition with nothing to say records nothing.

**Output.** At a candidate a (formulas.md), the output is the entered record's if the timeline has one at a; otherwise what the first row that gives something gives, where for a tag whose rows are all `←`, any row that holds; otherwise nothing is recorded. An entered record is narrower than any formula's pattern, so **entered wins** is the narrowest rule again.

**Loops.** Definitions are worked out in the order in which they read each other. A loop is **monotone** if nothing on it can go down when what it reads goes up: a tag goes up when attested, a number when it grows. AND, OR, ANY, IN, FILTER, a dot, a relation call, SUM, COUNT, MAX, MIN, and `+`, `*`, `>` and `>=` of amounts that aren't negative are monotone; NOT, ISBLANK, `-`, `/`, `<`, `=`, a row giving FALSE, and an IF whose condition is on the loop are not.

**The computed state.** Group by group, the least set of records with these outputs, worked out round by round from nothing until a round changes nothing. A loop that isn't monotone, or whose numbers never settle, shows `#CIRCULAR!`. A computed record's **stage** is the round in which it first appeared.

**Ground.** The ground of an expression where it gives something is: for a record it reads, that record; for AND, a comparison or arithmetic, the grounds of its parts; for IF, those of its condition and the branch taken; for OR and ANY, the ground of one part that holds, of least stage; for FILTER, SUM, COUNT and EACH, each item counted with the ground of its condition or value; for MAX and MIN, the item chosen and every argument that was nothing. A computed record's ground is the label of the row that gave it with that row's ground. Every computed record in a ground comes from an earlier group or round, so unfolding grounds always ends at entered records.

`WITHOUT(x, c)` holds where c holds in the computed state of the timeline without x and its dependents. On a tag r, `r.[label]` is TRUE where a row with that label holds.

The language is formulas.md's, less REACH, LOOKTHROUGH, IMPLIEDBY, IFS, NEXTDATE and ISRULEDOUT, which are definitions of one or two rows now. Look-through ownership, for instance:

```
= Ownership(Investor, Investment).Percentage
  + SUM(EACH(Investor.Holdings, @.Percentage * [Look-Through Ownership](@, Investment)))
```

### Views

A **view** is an object of the kind View. Its notes say how it presents the chart as of its day (today, unless it keeps one): its own (Name, Order, Layout, As Of, Page), and the notes in it. A view is the app's, not a fact about the world, so it's never dated. It has a **layout**: a **chart**, drawn on a page of a size; or a **table** whose rows are the records of one kind, one relation, or every entry of one datatype.

**Columns.** A table's **columns** are the patterns below its rows: each a path of edges from the row down, with every specification there, and a relation's sides. In the view, a Shown note at a column's pattern shows or hides it (by default each entry at the row shows), and Order notes place the columns. A Shown note in the view at a pattern below the rows where no record is yet is a column too, with no values, titled by its edge; the first value typed into it says what it holds, as the first values pasted under a new heading do. So inserting a column is one change, its Shown and Order notes; renaming one relabels its entry, as any rename does; and a column with no values goes with its notes.

**Marks.** In a chart, a record of the computed state has a **mark**: an object a **box**; a relation with two locations a **line**; any other record a **text**, in the box or on the line of its nearest location that has one. Marks nest as records do. A relation whose Connector is None is a text instead, in the box at its lower end (the side not Above), by role: "Directors: Margaret A. Chen, Priya N. Raman".

**Styles.** A **style** in a view is a pattern p with a map from p's outputs to **formats**: the view's format notes at "p with output o", for each output o. This is the user's definition. A format given at a record sets each property on the nearest mark, up the tree, that has it: Fill given at Entity Type: Limited Partnership fills that entity's box. One given at an entry's option or tag is a look of what has it, as a filter's Shown is: it sets the box or line the entry is in, never the entry's own text, so Bold given at Jurisdiction: Delaware bolds every Delaware box, whether or not it writes its jurisdiction; but Format, how a text reads, is the entry's own.

**Settings.** A **setting** is a mark and one of its properties. Its value is the first that applies: entered for that one record in this view (narrowest); for a position, the layout's; the styles'; the default (the widest note, in no view). Entered wins, as everywhere.

**Shown** is a property of every mark. A mark is **drawn** when it is shown and the marks it sits in or joins are drawn. A style showing or hiding by output is a view's filter; a condition worth filtering by is a tag of the chart, computed or entered. What's made on a chart, the chart shows: a box made on a chart view that the view would draw nowhere (its filter hides the kind) is shown there by the same change, a Shown note at its own tree, the narrowest.

**Fields.** The texts in a box, or on a line, are its **fields**: the entries of its record that are shown, in their entries' order, then its free text. A field is shown or hidden by a Shown note at the entry's pattern at a **scope**: at the box's object (this box: EIN at Fund III), at its kind (every entity: EIN at Entity), or, for a group of boxes alike by an option or a tag of theirs, at that output with the field's label as the note's param, the field *beside* it (EIN beside Vehicle Type: Main Fund), which reaches the field in each box a record fitting the output is in, as a format given there lands on that box. As for every setting, the narrowest decides: one box's own before a group's; a field beside an output before one at the entry alone, as it is the entry in those boxes only; between groups, the later style. A note at the entry wherever it is (Jurisdiction, at no kind) shows it where there is one; a note at a scope names a field of each box there, so where the record has none yet (an EIN not yet typed) the field is **blank**: its entry's name stands in its place, faint, to type into, in a box selected, pointed at or in the scope chosen; elsewhere the box keeps its room empty, as the PDF and the deck do. A blank may be of an entry the chart has at another kind (a Jurisdiction shown in a person's box), which the kind takes as it's typed, as Add gives it. A field's look is given at its entry's pattern, as any text's is.

**Free text.** A **Text** note at a scope gives each box or line it reaches one more text, after its fields. Its value is { text, …look }, and an `{Entry}` in its text reads as that entry of the mark's record reads, as a form letter's field does ("Formed {Formation Date}" reads "Formed February 14, 2023"), or as nothing where the record has none. It lands as a format does and the narrowest decides, so a box's own takes the place of its group's, and an empty one gives none.

**One change per act.** Showing or hiding a field, by any way the window has (the inspector's Show on chart, its row dragged onto a box, a copy and paste, the box's menu), writes its Shown notes at the scope, with the entries it's in where those are hidden; writing, editing or taking away a free text writes its Text note there. Each is one change, and Undo takes it back whole.

**Frames.** A format may draw a frame, a box or a bracket around the boxes it lands on, labeled by its caption: Jurisdiction: Cayman Islands draws "Offshore". Outputs of one style captioned alike share a frame, and a relation's frames the box at its lower end. Frames nest in the order of the styles.

**Free shapes.** A view's page also holds shapes bound to no record: text, a line or arrow, a picture, a frame. A text may be a formula (`="As of " & TEXT(@AsOf, "mmmm d, yyyy")`). A text box inserted goes in the view's empty room nearest the middle of the screen, with room for a line, clear of every box, line, label and the Key; made longer than that, it's placed again nearest where it was typed, so it never lands on what's drawn. Its place, as every box's, reads in the page's points from the page's top left.

**The legend** is a shape bound to the view's styles. Its rows are, for each style and each output from which a drawn mark takes a format, a swatch drawn in that format and its **caption** (the output, unless a Caption note says otherwise), outputs alike in format and caption sharing a row: a Caption note calls only the output it's at, and outputs alike with none are called by what they are ("AIV and Feeder Fund"). A row's outputs are in their option list's order, and the rows in the view's order of its styles, those of a style that is its one row (Names) after the rest. Styles lists the same rows, of every output, and the scope those that reach the selection, both in that order. It is moved, sized, formatted and edited like any shape.

**The layout** works out every position not entered, together: drawn boxes and their sizes, frames as clusters, the lines that rank boxes (a line's Above names the edge drawn on top, as Owner), and the routes of lines. **Tidy** removes a view's entered positions. A box placed among entered ones goes beside its relatives, on no line it isn't on. A highlight keeps every box where it is, and places what it reveals the same way; a redline is laid out as the change, made, will be. Boxes in a rank share a height, and triangles of a width and a size of name a size. A box's lines of one relation to every box in a frame land on the frame as one. The layout reads the relations, never their looks, so restyling moves nothing; lines alike along one route are drawn as one. No line runs through a box it doesn't end at, or through a caption: one that would goes around what's drawn. No line reads as another's: none turns down close under a box it doesn't come from (a lane's depth under it, within its width and 6 pt), unless the turn is as close under its own box, as a lane's turn under it is: a lane whose lines turn down under the box beside its own runs a lane's depth under that box, the layout leaving it the room where it placed the boxes; and no two run side by side within 8 pt, or over each other unless they share a box; a line that would goes around, leaving its box anywhere along the edge it leaves by rather than taking a step, and Tidy keeps a line that passes a tier straight, so no box is put across it. A line that goes around takes no lane, so no gap deepens and no other line moves for it, and it keeps out of the places of the labels of the lines it crosses where it can without more turns; labels are placed in reading order, so the order lines were made in decides none, and a line added disturbs only what it touches. A fund's owners' lines meet its apex and it sits under them, their percentages typed or not; its general partner's meets its side, from beside them. A trunk with a branch just beside where it leaves its box leaves it down that branch, rather than stepping aside to it. A label goes on its own line, with no other line as near it, where nothing else is drawn, 3 pt from any box as printed, and the layout leaves room for it. Arrow Auto draws an arrowhead where a line doesn't run down from its Above side. A role written in a box hangs where it runs on, as a list's entry does.

**The page** keeps its size's proportions, a 16:9 slide unless a Page note says otherwise, and grows as a whole to hold what's drawn, so the chart is drawn smaller to fit the slide, by twentieths, so its sizes stay on PowerPoint's half points, and set 24 pt under its title and date, as a document's text is under its heading, the room it doesn't take at its foot. Its Key goes at the chart's foot, where that sets it least far down, or beside the title where that draws the chart larger, and isn't drawn while nothing is keyed. Its title, date and Key are the sheet's own, at the sheet's size (20, 11 and 8 pt), however small the chart is drawn, but nothing in the Key is set larger than the names it explains. On a paper narrower than its page, its boxes are drawn closer, and a box whose one line dropped straight drops straight where the room beside it allows. A size picked is the slide's: as picked where the chart can keep a scale for it, or else as near as the page allows, never shrinking the chart more than holding its scale would.

**The scene** is the drawn marks and free shapes, each setting valued and each text broken into lines. It depends on the computed state and the view and nothing else, and the canvas, PDF and PowerPoint each draw it and nothing else.

### Workbook

A **workbook** is a list of named charts, each with its own schema and timeline, sharing nothing. A **session**, one browser tab, holds the open chart, the view, the selection and the time. It is in no log.

A new chart, in a workbook or a new one, is begun from the chart shown: its schema, its notes at no record (the definitions, names and requirements), its tables, and one chart view in **the house look**, whatever view is shown. The house look is the looks of the first chart view (its main page, Structure): its notes there, at no record, of every format property, Text and Order, but those at a relation a formula gives (§57's affiliates); of its Shown notes, those of fields and those keeping a kind off the page (Documents), never a filter; and the Captions of what it keeps; not the day it keeps. Its chart then draws every relation it records, and every box made in it.

## Part 2. The window

The window has three coordinates, and every screen is a setting of them:
- **the view**: what you are looking at;
- **the selection**: what you are asking about, as keys: a record's id, a free shape's, `legend#row` for a legend's row, `frame:/…` for a frame, `id@box` for a relation's text in one box, and `~pattern` for an entry, relation or kind in general;
- **the time**: today, a day in the world's time, or a moment in the log.

```
┌ ⌥ Calder Ridge Fund III ▾ ────────── Find, or type =   Ctrl K ───────── As of today ▾  Undo Redo  Export ┐
│ Insert ▾ │ This box │ All Funds ▾ │ Arial ▾ 10 ▾ │ B I U S A ▾ ≡ ▾ │ Shape ▾ Fill ▾ Outline ▾ │ Arrange ▾    │
│ ☑ Control  GP III → Halvard   More than 25% ← SUM(FILTER(Controlled.Owners, …)) > 25%                   │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                         │ the selection                  │
│                     the view: a chart on its page, or a table           │                                │
│                                                                         │                                │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┤
│ Structure │ 40 Act │ Post-closing │ Entities │ Ownership │ Dates │ Documents │ +     2 overdue · 4 blank   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**The title bar** holds the chart's name, renamed in place as a sheet is, and the chart menu (renaming the chart shown; a new chart, in the house look; a copy of the one shown, its items under new ids and what refers to them following; and deleting it, which its toast takes back, or, for a workbook's only chart, greyed, saying why; then the workbook's charts and the workbooks this browser keeps, each a list of its own, so nothing moves as they grow, with a new workbook and deleting one not open, after a confirm, as that can't be undone; files, History, Settings, light and dark, the tour), Find, the date, the inspector's button, Undo and Redo, and Export; Ctrl+P is Export as PDF.

**The format bar** shows on a chart, as PowerPoint's Home tab: the font, found as its name is typed, the chart's own first, then every font by its class, each in its own face, one not on this computer greyed; the size, typed or picked, Grow and Shrink stepping through PowerPoint's; bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, color; the paragraph's alignment, position and spacing; the shape and its shadow; fill and transparency; outline, its weight, dashes and a line's arrows; each color's eyedropper, taking the color of a mark clicked; arrange; and the format painter and Clear formatting. A choice pointed at is previewed on the chart.

**The scope** says what a change goes to, in two parts. On the left is **the selection**, as in PowerPoint ("This box", "These 3 boxes"): a change to it writes a note at each selected record's own tree in the view (a free shape keeps its look in its row). On the right is **all like it**, a **group**: a set of patterns, its **outputs**, each reaching the marks with a record in or at them that fits it. A change to a group writes a note at each output in the view, so it becomes a Styles row, and a row of the Key where it tells marks apart. A look given a group is what each of its marks then has: where a later style gives some of them otherwise (Funds' fill over the limited partnerships'), the same change puts the group's style after those in Styles, as Styles' own drag does, where that changes nothing else drawn, and says so; otherwise it says which style still decides. The groups of a selection are those that reach every mark of it, or for the view's Styles rows any: the Styles rows, each the outputs that look alike and are called alike (as the Key has them); and the selection's own, each one pattern: its kind (an object's) or its relation (a line's), and each option or tag of an entry in it, at any depth, the entry's tree with every object made any object and every other output taken away (Jurisdiction: Delaware; Not Good Standing). A group is named by its caption, or what it is, and its reach ("Funds (6)", "Jurisdiction: Delaware (13)"); one of its own a Styles row already is alone, is offered once, as that row. All like it names the group chosen, or else the narrowest, of those reaching more than the selection: the narrowest Styles row that styles boxes or lines, else the narrowest of its options, else its kind or relation. While a group is chosen or pointed at, the chart **cues** it: what it reaches lit, the rest dulled; drawn over the scene, never part of it. Another selection is formatted as itself again. A Key row or a frame selected is formatted as the whole Styles row it's drawn from. Ctrl+Shift+A goes from one part to the other.

**The look** of a mark is the value of each of its look properties: its fill, transparency, outline, weight, dash, shadow, text position and arrows, and its first text's font, size (the slide's), bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, color, alignment and spacing; never its shape, corners, format, frame, layer or route, which say what it is and where, as PowerPoint's painter copies none of them. **The format painter** copies a look (Ctrl+Shift+C) and pastes it (Ctrl+Shift+V), or, armed by its brush, on the mark clicked, at the scope (all like the mark, where a group was chosen as it was armed): a note of each look property some mark there doesn't already take so, with the look's value, as the bar writes it; one change. **Clear formatting** removes, as one change, every note of a look property at the scope in the view: for the selection, those at the trees of its records and the records in them; for a group, those at its outputs; and says what it removed.

**The formula bar** is one row, as Excel's is: the selection's output, its name, and the row that gave it; with nothing selected, the view's name. Its definition's rows drop over the view (the chevron, or Ctrl+Shift+U), headed by why, in a sentence of the facts that fired, each one a link ("… is an owner of the BDC with a voting percentage of 7% (5% or more), so it is an affiliated person (§2(a)(3)(A)), so it is a remote affiliate (§57(e)(1)(A))"); a result ruled out says the condition that held, then, after "but", what failed ("… signs for Fund III with a capacity of Managing Member, but is a member, not a managing member, of GP III, so the capacity is not held"); an item a list found is one fact, named by its kind, with what else was read of it ("AIV-1 is a limited partnership and has a Certificate of Limited Partnership with a status of Filed, so its formation document is on file"); a condition that only excludes (not a portfolio company) is no reason, and isn't said; a value says whose it is, at a relation by its sides, each said once ("Fund III's annual tax for 2026 is due June 1, 2027"), or "the" once the sentence has said the relation (the capacity), and a name that ends in a parenthesis with "of" ("the voting percentage of Fund III-A Investors (U.S. ERISA Plans) in Fund III-A"); what fell due before the day shown was due ("AIV-1's annual tax for 2025 was due June 1, 2026"), and the last of what was done was then ("the Manager's last annual amendment was on March 28, 2026"); a date moved off a weekend says the day it fell on ("… is due January 29, 2027 (January 31, a Sunday, so the Friday before)"); only a section is cited, never a row's label; the rows that hold are in ink and the rest grey. Each name in a row takes a color, and what it means here is outlined on the chart in that color; deeper reasons are the same hue, lighter, down to entered records, even ones the view hides. Hovering part of a formula shows its value here. Clicking a computed line steps down into the clause its parent cited, with a breadcrumb back. The definition is edited here.

**The view.** Views are sheet tabs, dragged to reorder. A chart view is a page of shapes: select, marquee, move, resize, guides, align, order, group, nudge, copy and paste, the format painter, as in PowerPoint. A box's texts are its fields, as a form's are (see Fields): pointing at one outlines it, with the text pointer and its entry's name; a click on one in a box already selected selects it, as a second click into a PowerPoint shape goes into its text, and a double-click anywhere, F2, Enter or a key typed edits it in place (Tab keeps it and goes on to the next, as a form's does), the same record its inspector row edits, which lights as the field is pointed at or edited, and the other way round. Delete clears a field selected, as it clears a cell: its value entered goes, one change, and the field stays, a blank where a scope shows it; its eye in the inspector, or its menu, hides it, from this box, or from all like it, the group whose note shows it there, so what showed it is what's taken back; Ctrl+C copies it, and Ctrl+V on another box shows that box's own entry there. Where a narrower note, or a later group's, keeps some of a group's boxes as they were, the toast says which. A double-click in a selected box's own room, or Add text, begins a free text, where typing `{` offers the record's entries. Pointing at a box shows its handles, as Lucidchart's: one dragged to another box shows what holds between the two, in sentences, with any other relation they could be in one step away, where ticking one is a change pending through the bar; dragged into empty room, it makes a box there, named as it's typed, and asks the same of the two; Esc makes nothing. A box made on the chart (so, Insert's, or Add a box's) is named in place, its kind beside its name, the one picked or else the one last made, which Tab or a click there switches; Enter makes it. Delete deletes what's selected, a box or a line as the inspector's Delete… does, a change pending that asks when it ends; its menu has Hide in this view, and History, which opens filtered to it. Select all like this (Ctrl+Shift+Space) selects every mark of the narrowest group the selection is in, as the Key and Styles group them. The pointer says what a click does: the text pointer over a field, move over a selected box, the crosshair while relating. A table view is a grid edited like Excel, pasting included (and Import, a file of Excel's or CSV read as a paste with a heading row), that scrolls across under its frozen columns (the first, unless the user freezes more or none) rather than cut a name. Its keys are Excel's: the arrows, Enter and Tab move (Enter back to the column a run of Tabs began in), typing or F2 edits, Delete clears, Ctrl+D and Ctrl+R fill down and right, Ctrl+Enter keeps what's typed in every cell selected, Ctrl+Space and Shift+Space select the column and the row. A heading dragged moves its column at once; its menu (its chevron, a right-click, or Alt+Down) sorts by what the column holds, filters it as Excel does, by its values, inserts a column named as it's typed, renames, hides, moves, freezes, fits and groups by it; the Columns list above the grid shows, hides and reorders every column. Under the last row, a new one: a name typed there adds a record of the table's kind, and a relation's asks its sides, then waits on Enter as any relation added does. The status bar counts, sums and averages the cells selected. How one person sorts, filters, groups, freezes and sizes a table is theirs, kept per view in their browser, and no change. A table exports to Excel, a number a number, a percentage a percentage and a date a date, its columns in their order and widths, or to CSV.

**The inspector** shows whatever is selected:

| Selected | It shows |
| --- | --- |
| nothing | the view: its layout; a chart's styles and what it hides one by one, each with Show; a table's columns |
| a box or a row | the record: its kind and its first option, which open the kind's table and select every box with that option; its entries as a tree, each a field of its box, with Show on chart at its end, dragged or copied onto a box to show it there; then its relatives by list, then its documents; its look and position, or, where the chart shown hides it, that it's hidden there, with Show; and behind ⋯ at its top right, what its box's right-click has that isn't the chart's arranging, in sections, none empty: its table, every box of its option, its History; Hide or Show in this view; Delete…. Add offers what it could have: entries, and its kind's lists (Owners, General Partners), each asking who, one the chart has or a new one named as it's typed |
| a line | the relation: its sides, the day it ends on where that's after the day shown (a link to the chart then), its output, what's at it; behind ⋯, its History, Hide or Show in this view, Transfer… and Delete…; and, quietly beside Add, Transfer… |
| two boxes | what holds between them, as sentences ("Fund III is an owner of Fund III Blocker"), and Add a relation |
| a column heading, a legend row, an entry's label | that entry or relation in general: its name, meaning, options, required, start and end, styles, and its definition set out as the statute it encodes (each clause's words beside its rows, and what it gives) |

It is always beside the view, on a chart or a table, so nothing moves under the pointer as the selection changes; only the user puts it away (its button, Ctrl+\, or its divider dragged to the window's edge) and brings it back. Widened, the inspector is the record's page, with its way back at its top left, named for the view it's over, as the view's tab, the panel's button and Esc are too. Where two people gave one value at once, it shows both answers, each with who gave it; keeping one settles it. History, when open, takes its place.

**Pending changes are redlined** before they are made, as lawyers' comparison software shows them, and exactly as Enter will make them: the chart drawn is the chart Enter draws, its boxes where they will stand and its lines on their routes, with what would appear in blue, a value that would change in blue under what it said, struck in red, and what would go struck through in red where it is, the view showing only what it shows. Hovering a tag, typing a value or a formula, adding a relation, deleting, transferring and pasting all preview this way; Enter commits, Esc discards. A change that waits on Enter (a relation added, ticked or newly named, a deletion, a transfer, a paste) has the window's one bar under the view, saying what it does, in the redline's ink, and when it holds, with Discard and its verb; the inspector shows it too, a record being deleted or transferred whole struck and the end entry it's given in blue. It's confirmed where it was asked as well: under the two-box panel's row of a relation ticked or unticked, and atop the panel of a record being deleted or transferred, its day, Discard and its verb, the bar's state, taking the keys as it's begun. A struck label is drawn only where it's clear of what the change leaves. A click elsewhere, another view, another day or another chart leaves it pending. It's discarded only by Discard, its box unticked, or Esc from its controls or with nothing else to escape, the selection first; Undo discards it before anything else. However it's discarded, a toast says so ("Discarded adding Ownership: …"), with Redo, as the Redo key does until something else is made or begun, which brings it back as it was. Nothing drops it without a word: one begun in its place, its records going, or a reload says so.

**Time.** The date control shows the chart as of any day, with a strip of the days on which something starts or ends. Typing a value corrects it; with a day shown, it changes from that day. A start or an end typed past the day shown (a Formation Date) takes what it's at out of the chart then, which the redline strikes and the toast says. A relation added between two boxes with no start of its own, an ownership say, asks its day in its redline, before Enter: From the day shown, typed over, or All along. A record deleted asks the day it ends on the same way, the first day it no longer holds: Ends on the day shown, typed over, or Never held; one with an end entry asks that entry instead (Dissolution Date). A relation transferred (Transfer…, in its panel's ⋯, its line's right-click and quietly in its panel) asks, set out as History will say it, from whom to whom ("From Fund III Blocker to [ ▾ ]", a search of who fits that side, or a new one named as it's typed), how much ("[82%] of Halvard Logistics Holdings, Inc.", all of it till a part is typed) and On the day shown, typed over; whom is asked at once, and picked, the keys go on to how much, then the day. A day said after the day shown is a link to the chart then, where the change is drawn; a change that asks no day holds from the day shown when it was begun. History, Undo and Redo say the day an added relation holds from, or all along; a deletion as it was asked, "Ended Ownership: … on Nov 1, 2026", or "Deleted Ownership: … as never held"; and a transfer as it was asked. A filing or payment recorded beside its deadline takes effect from the day it was made. History lists the log, a change and its Undo as one, struck, and shows every view as it stood after a change, which can be undone from there; and what's deleted, each with Restore. With something selected it lists what touches that, so History in a box's or a line's menu, or its panel's ⋯, is that record's.

**Moving.** Find (Ctrl+K) reaches any record, list, view or action, and `=` works a formula out at the selection. Alt+← and Alt+→ walk the trail of selections. The status bar counts what needs attention (overdue, blank where required, errors), and F8 steps through them all. A count goes to the first of its kind, and clicked again to the next.

**Launch.** The dappled wall shows once per opened tab and dims into the page. On the very first launch a tour of five steps follows, each advanced by doing what it teaches: select a box; drag to another; select an italic value; point at a tag; switch views.

## Part 3. The look and the words

**Words.** No subtitles, descriptions, hints or tips. An empty state is a few words and its one action. A tooltip names an icon-only control and its key. A placeholder is "Find", or "New" and what it makes ("New option", "New relation", "New entity", "New column"), never an example or an instruction. A toast is a verb, an object and Undo. Nothing on screen says demo or sample. Teaching belongs to the tour. The model's terms (specification, pattern, note, candidate) never appear. Where a relation is, it's named by its sides, two as their line joins them (Fund III → Fund III Blocker), three or more as its sentence says them (Margaret A. Chen for Fund III, via GP III), the same everywhere.

**Computed** is italic, a computed tag's box is outlined, and a computed line in the default style is dashed: on screen only, like Excel's trace arrows. An entered value that says other than its formula keeps a small amber corner; one that agrees, a paid tax year say, overrides nothing and has none. Errors show in place.

**Type.** IBM Plex Sans throughout, formulas included, with their functions in small capitals; four sizes; weights 400, 500, 600. The chart's text is the document's: Arial by default, which PowerPoint and the PDF draw alike, or any of forty fonts from Office, Windows and the Mac, each drawn where it isn't installed by the fonts of its metrics (Calibri by Carlito), or any font typed. PowerPoint names the font itself. The PDF embeds the face the canvas draws, this computer's, with only the letters used, once the browser lets its fonts be read (it asks once); a face with a standard font's widths is in that font (Arial in Helvetica, Times New Roman in Times, Courier New in Courier), and one that can't be read or embedded in the standard font of its class, which saving says ("with Garamond printed as Times"). A line its font would set wider than the scene is condensed to the scene's width, so no text leaves its box. Icons are Lucide's, familiar ones, few, with menus in words.

**Color.** Warm paper and ink, hairlines rather than cards or shadows, and one slate-navy accent for the primary action, the selection and focus. Green attested, red ruled out or overdue, amber needs attention. Dark is tuned on its own; the chart's page stays white.

**The mark** is a tree grown by one rule, each limb ending in two, with its roots the same rule reflected below.
