# Dendra

Dendra is a system of record for the legal team of a private fund manager, or any team that keeps track of entities. It keeps their entities, people and investor groups; the ownership, control, positions and signing authority between them; their registrations, deadlines and documents. It works out by formula what follows from these, draws structure charts from them, and exports the charts to PowerPoint and PDF.

This document is the design. Part 1 continues [data-model.md](data-model.md), whose terms it uses throughout, and builds the rest of the model from them in the same way. Part 2 is the window. Part 3 is the look and the words. Part 4 is what it's built with.

## Part 1. The model

### Time

A **period** is a finite union of spans of days, each "since a, before b", where either end may be absent.

A **dated record** is a record together with a nonempty period, its **validity**. A **timeline** in a schema S is a finite set of dated records of S in which no two distinct records with the same argumentation are valid on a common day, unless both are objects.

A record of a timeline **holds** on a day d if it is valid on d and each of its locations holds on d. The records that hold on d are **the chart as of d**. Sanity check: the chart as of d is a state. It is closed under locations by the definition of holding, and two of its records with one argumentation would both be valid on d.

A kind or relation may name a date entry at it as its **start** and another as its **end** (see Notes): an Entity's are Formation Date and Dissolution Date, a General Partner's Admission Date and Withdrawal Date. A record's validity is then its own period narrowed to the days from its start, if it has one, and before its end. So Formation Date stays an ordinary field, and a fund is in the chart as of a day only once it's formed. A kind may name more than one end, each its own act: an Entity's Merger Date ends it as its Dissolution Date does, by a merger, a relation of the entity merged and its survivor (Merged Into, from the entity merged), which takes one entity, as a check says. Like all that's at the entity, the relation holds until the day it ends.

So a record added holds from its start, whenever it's recorded: a fund added today is in the chart from its Formation Date, and an object with no start, a person, is in it all along. A relation with none, an ownership or a membership, has only its own period, so the day it begins is said as it's added: the day it began, or none.

**A date is evidence.** A date entry says what's known of a day, and no more: a day (2022-03-14), or only its month or its year (2022-03, 2022), as ISO writes them, typed Mar 2022, March 2022, 3/2022 or 2022. It's kept so and read so, on the chart, in a table and in the file (March 2022), and it's worked out from its first day, by the letters of ISO, so a fund formed in March 2022 is in the chart from March 1 and no sooner. Nothing invents a day: a formula's date that only reads it reads as it's known, and a format that asks its day leaves that out. **Today is never evidence.** Nobody who adds a relation at today has said that's when it began; they've said it holds. So a relation added with no day said holds **all along**, which is exactly that: no day was given; it holds on every day its sides do, as today's knowledge of them, and reads All along, never a day its side began on. Where a person has gone to another day and added it there, it holds from that day, which they asked for, and from any day typed. All along is the honest default as well as the convenient one: a chart built in an evening from an email reads as a chart of what the email says, on every day, and not as a history of that evening's typing, and a date said later narrows it. For the same reason a blank start entry isn't offered today, because a stake was made today. A value that doesn't read as what's asked is ringed in red and says why in words, wherever it's typed.

A record deleted is the mirror. One whose kind or relation has an end entry ends by it, whenever that's recorded: deleting a fund gives it its Dissolution Date, a general partner its Withdrawal Date. Any other object or relation, an ownership say, ends on a day said as it's deleted: its period ends that day, with all that's at it, and it stays in the chart as of every day before. Or it never held, a mistake, and is deleted as if never made. So deleting today an owner who redeemed today leaves last year's chart as it was. The day a record **ends on**, by its period or its end entry, is the first day it no longer holds, everywhere it's said.

A relation of two sides that's entered and holds, not one a formula gives, can be **transferred**: from its first side, the holder its sentence begins with ("Fund III Blocker is an owner of …"), to another record that fits there, one the chart has or a new one; on a day; all of it, or, where it has an **amount**, the first figure entered at it in its entries' order (an ownership's Percentage), a part. A figure entered beside the amount that says the same (a Voting Percentage of the same 82%) is the amount said again. A transfer is one change, read in the chart as of its day, and writes:
- what it's from: all of it, the relation ends on the day, as deleting it ends it; a part, its amount, and each figure saying it again, less the part, from the day;
- what it's to: where the other record holds a relation of the same name and output with the same other side on the day, that one's amount, and each figure saying it again, more the part, from the day; or else a new relation of the same output, the other record at the first side and the other side as it was, holding from the day (its start entry given the day, where its relation has one), with the part as its amount and its figures saying it again, and a copy of every other entry entered at the old one but its dates, which were the old one's own; and a record new with it, of a kind its side takes, named, holding from the day.

History reads a transfer back from these records alone, as "Transferred 82% of Halvard Logistics Holdings, Inc. from Fund III Blocker to Northwind Holdings LLC on Nov 1, 2026", one without an amount by its option or name ("Transferred General Partner of Fund III from GP III to GP IV on …").

A record is **inserted between** the sides of a relation whose amount is a percentage, where a record of its kind can hold what the relation holds and be held by what holds it: on a day, the relation is transferred whole to that record, a new one or one the chart has, and a relation of the same name and output runs from the old holder to it at 100%, with each figure that said the amount again. It's one change, read back from those records alone: "Inserted Holdco between Fund III and Fund III Blocker on Jan 1, 2027". What's held stays held all the way up, so what a formula gives through the chain stands.

Whoever's **responsible** for a record is a relation of two sides, Responsible, a person, and For, an entity or a date's own record (the tag or the relation a deadline is at), which no chart draws unless asked, or an entry of that name, in its words, on a chart that keeps it so. A deadline is on the hook of the nearest up from it: its own record's, or else its entity's.

A record's **kind** can be changed, every version of it, one change, to one its relations still fit, on whatever day they hold; what's noted at it is noted at its id, and stays. A person named as a company is made one without being made again; where that leaves it a box of no form, its toast says what Export will ("Pinecrest Capital has no Entity Type"), a click from that row.

A tax owed **year by year** is a relation of what owes it and a year, an object of the kind Tax Year in the chart from its first day. A definition gives it for each year from the one the record starts in, so a record with no start owes none. Each year's due date is worked out at the relation until the entry its rule waits on is entered there: a Delaware entity's Annual Tax Due until its Annual Tax Paid, due March 1 after the year for a corporation (its franchise tax) and June 1 for a limited partnership or LLC. A **payment** made on a day writes that entry, with the day, at the earliest year of the relation still due for the same first side, entering that year's relation to bear it; both hold from the day. So every payment is kept with its day, at the year it settles, and none overwrites another. The next due date is the following year's, so a tax paid early moves the next due date on, and it appears once that year begins. A payment is said by the year it settles: "Paid Fund III Blocker's annual tax for 2026 on Oct 28, 2026".

### Change

An **item** is anything in a workbook with an identity: a chart, a specification of its schema (so a kind or an option can exist before any record has it), a record, a note or a free shape. A **row** is a version of an item; a dated record's row carries its validity.

A **change** removes some rows and adds others, and says who made it and when, and may say why, in a **note** of its own that History shows beside it, and what it did, where it's one act of many rows ("Gave 40 Act the look of Structure"). A reason can be given afterwards, by a change that removes and adds nothing and says what it's **about**, with who gave it and when: nothing already in the log is touched, and as the log stood before then, it hadn't been given; its reverse takes it back. A **log** is a set of changes in which every row removed was added by a change in the log. A row is **live** if it was added and not removed. **A workbook is its log**: its timeline, notes, views and charts are its live rows.

The **reverse** of a change removes the rows it added and adds fresh copies of the rows it removed; it can be made while every row the change added is live. **Undo** reverses your latest change not yet reversed, and **Redo** your latest undo, if you have made no change since. An item is **deleted** when it has rows in the log and none live. **Restoring** it adds fresh copies of what the change that deleted it removed of it and of what was at it, and nothing else that change did; it can be made while what the item is at is live.

A change can be **changed**, and the log still only grows. An **amendment** is one change that says which it **amends**, and it's that change's act made again, on another day, of another amount or to another party: it takes back what the first recorded, its records' rows alone, as its reverse would, what it placed and how it looked standing; and it writes the act as it now is, the records it makes keeping the ids they had, so what's at them since stays at them, and a box stays where it stands. Its reverse brings the first back as it was, so Undo takes back the amendment and nothing else. So nothing's rewritten: the log as it stood before the amendment still has the first, and the chart as it stood then reads as it did. A transfer, an insertion and a merger, each read back from its records alone, are amended so, each only while every record row it added is live: a later change that took one has to be undone first, which is said, by name, as Undo says it; and what's recorded since at what would then have no row goes with it, by name, as Undo's "Takes with it" says. A day it can't be made on, as what it's of doesn't hold then, is said on its bar, by name, and not taken. **What the act now is** is read as the act was, from the records it adds and those the first took away, so an amendment reads as a transfer does ("Transferred 15% of Halvard Holdco, LLC from Fund III Blocker to Northstar on Dec 20, 2026"), and amended again it's the same act again. A value typed from a day, and the day a relation holds from or ends on, have no act to read back and need nothing reversed: an amendment gives those rows another day or another value, the rows either side of the day meeting where they met, so a later value that rests on them keeps its own day; a day has to fall between the days either side.

The **union** of two logs is a log, and taking unions is commutative, associative and idempotent. Saving anywhere is therefore: read the copy there, take the union, write it back. Live rows that break the timeline's condition **disagree**; only changes made without seeing each other can, and a person settles it by keeping one, which is a change.

The log as it stood at a moment keeps only the changes made by then. So a chart can be read two ways, independently: **as of a day** (the world's time) and **as it stood** at a moment (the time it was recorded).

### Notes

A **pattern** is a tree obtained from a record by replacing some of its objects with their kinds, its value (if it has one) with its datatype, and removing the labels of some of its nodes. A record **fits** each pattern obtained from it this way, and a pattern p is **narrower** than q if q can be obtained from p this way. A specification and a parameterization are patterns, and so is every record. A pattern may also leave a location **open**, all of what's below it taken away: any record fits there, whatever its shape, and a pattern with one is wider than any it could be filled in as.

So one relation can have a side of several kinds of record, its name a note at one pattern. **Evidence** is one: a document, and what it evidences, Evidenced, open. Its specifications say what can be evidenced, an ownership (of any owner), a position (of any option) or a signing authority; its name, its two lists (Evidences from the document, Evidenced By from what it evidences), its one table and its one column are the pattern's. Evidence read by a definition is one list wherever it's read (the Authorized Signatory row of Capacity Held), and a record it evidences reads it among its documents. It ends with what it evidences, and isn't what a transfer moves: the new holder's is a new certificate. Where such a side is shown, it's said by the place rule ("Margaret A. Chen for Fund III, via GP III"), and can't be typed there by name.

**View** is a kind every chart has, the app's own, never listed among the chart's kinds.

A **note** is an entry located at a pattern, and optionally also at a view, labeled by one of the app's **note labels**:

Renaming relabels every tree in the chart, records, schema and the patterns of notes alike, so nothing needs re-keying. Moving an entry up a level is one substitution in every tree.

### Definitions

A Formula note is a **definition**. It is a list of **rows**, each a label and a formula, and it covers the records fitting its pattern. `← c` is short for `= IF(c, TRUE)`.

**Nothing.** BLANK is nothing, and nothing is worked out from nothing. It drops out of a sum of what's read and wipes out a product. A number written in the formula only moves what's read, so nothing moved by it is nothing: the year after a blank one, or a day before it. A date of nothing is nothing, whatever month and day are written beside it, as YEAR, EDATE, EOMONTH and WORKDAY of nothing are; a date of something and nothing, a year with no month, can't be made. Nothing is empty, so it equals 0, empty text and FALSE, as in Excel; but it has no size and no day, so a comparison of order with it is nothing: no tax year is on or after a formation date not recorded. SUM, MIN and MAX of nothing are nothing, and COUNT is 0. A definition with nothing to say records nothing.

**Output.** At a candidate a (formulas.md), the output is the entered record's if the timeline has one at a; otherwise what the first row that gives something gives, where for a tag whose rows are all `←`, any row that holds; otherwise nothing is recorded. An entered record is narrower than any formula's pattern, so **entered wins** is the narrowest rule again.

**Not tested.** A row can say yes, no or either of a tag: `←` only yes, IF what its branches say, LET what its end says, a literal itself, and anything worked out either. A definition whose rows can say both, and that says neither at a record it covers (where its entry is specified at a record of that kind, whatever its options), has not tested that record, every row's condition having skipped it: it reads **n/a**, apart from no and from a blank. One that can only say yes says no by its silence (not a private fund), and one that can only say no finds nothing wrong by it (Percentages In Range), each on hover in a table. So a portfolio company's Formation Document on File is n/a, not missing. A definition that can say both and says something of no record it covers **applies to no one**, which is no answer of anyone's being fine: its page says so ("It applies to no one: n/a at all 8 individuals"), and of an edit that would leave it so, where it applied to some, or a first formula, its preview and its Done say so, a warning apart from flaws, as Done stops for one and Save anyway, which asks why, makes it. Nothing that does that is said as what it took away, and it's never "works again". Any other change that would leave a check so says it too ("D&O Questionnaire Current would apply to no one").

**Can't say.** n/a means a rule doesn't apply. Where it applies and what it reads is blank, it hasn't answered: a row of a yes-or-no that can say no, come to a test of an entry that has no value at that record, **can't say**, in a state of its own, neither yes, no nor n/a, wherever its answer would be read. What decides is read in three values: a comparison of order with what's read blank can't be told wherever it stands, and an equality can't where it's the test (`[Status] = "Filed"`), but where it's a condition of the rule's, an IF's first argument, a blank is what it says, for a rule that asks only of Main Funds doesn't apply to what isn't known to be one. No beats it under AND, yes under OR; NOT keeps it; IF of what can't be told can't be told; ANY and ALL read it as OR and AND; LET reads through. So `[Date] >= X` needs no `NOT(ISBLANK(...))` to be right, and `NOT(ISBLANK([Date])) AND [Date] >= X`, which says a blank fails, says no. Blank here is an entry a hand could type at a record of that kind: what a definition gives is its own to say nothing of, and a list with nothing in it has nothing to want. Can't say is `#N/A`, as a value that can't be worked out for want of what it read is ("Needs Fiscal Year End"), which passes on to what reads it. It's no rule in error and no failure: the status bar counts it under blank, once for each field at each record however many rules want it, the field named ("D&O Questionnaire Date · Priya N. Raman", "D&O Questionnaire Current can't say"), going to which takes the keys to it; and its why says so ("Priya N. Raman's D&O Questionnaire Date is blank, so D&O Questionnaire Current can't say"). A chart draws it as what hasn't passed. What a check that says something at a record reads of it, where the kind has that entry and the record hasn't, shows as an empty row in its panel, so what's missing can be seen and typed.

**What a rule covers** is said on its page, counted, each a click from who: "1 holds · 1 fails · 1 can't say · 5 n/a", the n/a by what its silence means.

**Today.** `TODAY()` is the day shown: the day of the page or the working-out it's read in, whichever (see Views), not the clock's. So as of a day, a rule that reads today reads that day, on every page, in the list and in Export, each at its own day; on a tab that keeps a day, that day. No screen is half rolled forward. As it stood at a moment, the day shown is that moment's, so what was flagged is what was flagged then.

**A record by its name.** A name in a row that's no name of what the row is at, and that an object has as its Name or Short Name, on any day, reads as that object (`[Calder Ridge Capital BDC, Inc.]`, `[The BDC]`, the bare `[BDC]` too), so nothing has to compare a name as text: `@ = [The BDC]`. Suggestions offer it from a first letter, by any word of the name. Two things that have had one name are a flaw. Renamed, a record's name in every row is reworded with it in the one change, as a relation's name is, and the old name still reads as that record, as of a day it was so.

**Typed over.** What's typed over a rule's answer, a yes-or-no or any value it gives or could give otherwise, is a decision, kept in the open: its change asks why, Override not being made till it's given; it can have an **Until**, its row's period ending then, past which the rule says again; the way back, Back to the rule… or clearing it anywhere (the panel's value, a table's cell or cells), asks why as well, and the day it stops (or Never held), so what stood stood and as of a day before it still reads so. Override… is the command, in the formula bar, on the row's right-click, on a table cell's, and in Find by Waive, Waiver, Exception, Attest and Rule out; on what can't say, it enters a yes. Every override is on one list: Rules, and a count of its own in the status bar ("1 typed over"), its list saying what the rule says, why, and until when. Export's still-open line counts it. In Excel its cell is filled, what the rule says, why, who and until when as the cell's note.

**Ours to track.** What a definition works out as owed, a deadline, a tax, a document to have on file, is the chart's to track only for a record the chart says is its own, by an entry that says what the record is to it. A record not yet typed, or another's, owes nothing tracked and fails no such check. That entry, which a definition asks ISBLANK of, is required, so a record without it is counted blank, once: the one thing to fill in; but only once a record of the chart has it, so a chart with no funds isn't asked what vehicle each box is. In the sample it's an entity's Vehicle Type, other than Portfolio Company or Third Party: Northwind, a buyer, owes nothing tracked, and a new fund's taxes, filings and documents begin once it's typed a Main Fund.

**Blank by choice.** A blank that's out of scope and a blank that's forgotten aren't the same. An entry or a list of relatives blank at a record can be said **not ours**: a Required note saying no, at that record's own tree and the entry's or list's label, the narrowest rule again, by a change that can say why and that History reads as what it is ("Said Responsible of Northpine isn't ours"). It reads "Not ours", where it's looked for and in a file, and is neither counted nor listed as a blank to fill in. What isn't required is apart from what is: **blank, not required** is what at least half of a kind's records have and this one hasn't (not an end, which is blank till it ends, nor a short name), and who's responsible, where some of its kind have someone. It stands empty on the record's panel, to be typed, and is listed apart under Blank, so the status bar's count, of what's required, is never read as all that's empty, which its hover says.

**Loops.** Definitions are worked out in the order in which they read each other. A loop is **monotone** if nothing on it can go down when what it reads goes up: a tag goes up when attested, a number when it grows. AND, OR, ANY, IN, FILTER, a dot, a relation call, SUM, COUNT, MAX, MIN, and `+`, `*`, `>` and `>=` of amounts that aren't negative are monotone; NOT, ISBLANK, `-`, `/`, `<`, `=`, a row giving FALSE, and an IF whose condition is on the loop are not.

**The computed state.** Group by group, the least set of records with these outputs, worked out round by round from nothing until a round changes nothing. A loop that isn't monotone, or whose numbers never settle, shows `#CIRCULAR!`. A computed record's **stage** is the round in which it first appeared.

**A rule in error.** A definition can be wrong as it's written, which a wrong answer mustn't hide. A row has a **flaw** where it names what the chart has nowhere (`#NAME?`), or compares a choice with a quoted value that's none of its options, a kind with what's no kind, or what an object goes by (its Name or Short Name) with a quoted name no object has on the day shown, that isn't of what's out of the chart that day (`#OPTION?`: the value is read as that error, never as a comparison that's false, so "Deleware" isn't a clean no); or compares what can never be compared where what each side holds is known, a date with a number, or a date or a number with text (`#VALUE!`: "D&O Questionnaire Date is a date and 12 is a number: they can't be put in order"); any other free word compared that the entry doesn't have is only said; and a definition has one where it's on a loop that isn't monotone, or a monotone one each of whose rows gives something only through the loop, with nothing typed for it to go on from, so that it never holds (`#CIRCULAR!`). A role that two relations share, read by its label, reads both, which is said too, but isn't an error, for it may be what's meant. A rule with a flaw, or that failed by one of those errors when worked out, is **in error**, and so is every definition that reads one, at any remove: what they give stays worked out, but isn't an answer. A yes-or-no in error hasn't passed: it's drawn as one that doesn't hold, so a check that breaks flags everything it covers, and never clears its own flags. A value a record lacks (a `#N/A`) is no rule in error, but a rule waiting on a fact (see Can't say). Whatever can't be worked out, a rule in error or a `#VALUE!` the kinds couldn't tell, as a list where it wants one value, is a **rule broken**, counted in the status bar by the rule, apart from errors, which are who fails what a rule says. An edit that leaves one stops at Done whatever was there before, saying where ("It can't be worked out at Margaret A. Chen and Helen V. Ortiz: …"), and "works again" is said only of one that gives answers everywhere it should.

**Ground.** The ground of an expression where it gives something is: for a record it reads, that record; for AND, a comparison or arithmetic, the grounds of its parts; for IF, those of its condition and the branch taken; for OR and ANY, the ground of one part that holds, of least stage; for FILTER, SUM, COUNT and EACH, each item counted with the ground of its condition or value; for MAX and MIN, the item chosen and every argument that was nothing. A computed record's ground is the label of the row that gave it with that row's ground. Every computed record in a ground comes from an earlier group or round, so unfolding grounds always ends at entered records. A record that holds by more than one row has each for a ground, ordered by **steps**, fewest first, the rows' order between equals: a computed record a ground reads is a step, and that record's own nearest ground's steps are more, so a ground that reads only what's entered, the direct one, comes first (the Manager is the BDC's adviser, before it's controlled by one of the BDC's directors). A ground that leans on a record of its own round is told of too, after those that don't, but isn't what unfolding follows.

`WITHOUT(x, c)` holds where c holds in the computed state of the timeline without x and its dependents. On a tag r, `r.[label]` is TRUE where a row with that label holds. A relation's name, at a record of it, is the list of who's at its other sides there (`COUNT([Side Letter])`), as its lists are. `AND(a, b)` and `OR(a, b)` are `a AND b` and `a OR b`, as Excel writes them.

The language is formulas.md's, less REACH, LOOKTHROUGH, IMPLIEDBY, IFS, NEXTDATE and ISRULEDOUT, which are definitions of one or two rows now. Look-through ownership, for instance:

```
= Ownership(Investor, Investment).Percentage
  + SUM(EACH(Investor.Holdings, @.Percentage * [Look-Through Ownership](@, Investment)))
```

### Views

A **view** is an object of the kind View. Its notes say how it presents the chart as of its **day**: the day it keeps (its As Of), or else the session's, the day shown on a view that keeps none, today unless another is asked for; so nothing about the view you stand on changes another's. Its notes are its own (Name, Order, Layout, As Of, Page), and the notes in it. A view is the app's, not a fact about the world, so it's never dated. It has a **layout**: a **chart**, drawn on a page of a size; or a **table** whose rows are the records of one kind, one relation, or every entry of one datatype.

**Columns.** A table's **columns** are the patterns below its rows: each a path of edges from the row down, with every specification there, and a relation's sides. In the view, a Shown note at a column's pattern shows or hides it, whatever's shown above it (by default each entry at the row shows, but a tag a Check note covers, which is marked on the row it fails at and shows as a column only ticked), and Order notes place the columns. A column ticked or unticked is a look, per view in this browser, over those notes (see Workbook), till Keep these, in the Columns list, makes them the view's. A table made new opens with what names its rows and a few more, in its own order, the rest it would show hidden by the change that makes it. A Shown note in the view at a pattern below the rows where no record is yet is a column too, with no values, titled by its edge; the first value typed into it says what it holds, as the first values pasted under a new heading do. So inserting a column is one change, its Shown and Order notes; renaming one relabels its entry, as any rename does; and a column with no values goes with its notes. A table of a kind's records has a column too for each **list of relatives** a relation of two sides makes at its rows, or at an entry down from them (Registered Agents, the agents of a Principal at the row's Jurisdiction; Owners; Holdings): its pattern the relation's, its param the side listed, as a relation's side is a column; its cells the names of that side's records, as the record's panel lists them; hidden till shown. Typed into, a list's cell names the row's relatives in it: each record it names and the row isn't related to, one the chart has by its name as a name pasted finds one, or a new one of the kind the list's mostly are, is related, and each it no longer names ends, as one change pending, as a paste is, from the day asked; naming none, it's a deletion, which asks until when. Two lists of one name at a row, seen from one side, are told apart by what they reach, in their headings and the Columns list. A table of what's worked out (a relation a formula gives, or the dates) has a column **Why** too, hidden till shown: a Shown note at the view itself, Why its param; its cells each record's reason in plain words, every ground, as the formula bar says it, wrapped as a name is and written to its file. A list a formula gives a record opens as such a table, which is a look: its relation's rows, Why shown, in the table of them the chart has or a tab of this window's named for the list, filtered to that record where others have such lists.

**Marks.** In a chart, a record of the computed state has a **mark**: an object a **box**; a relation with two locations a **line**; any other record a **text**, in the box or on the line of its nearest location that has one. Marks nest as records do. A relation whose Connector is None is a text instead, in the box at its lower end (the side not Above), by role: "Directors: Margaret A. Chen, Priya N. Raman".

**Styles.** A **style** in a view is a pattern p with a map from p's outputs to **formats**: the view's format notes at "p with output o", for each output o. This is the user's definition. A format given at a record sets each property on the nearest mark, up the tree, that has it: Fill given at Entity Type: Limited Partnership fills that entity's box. One given at an entry's option or tag is a look of what has it, as a filter's Shown is: it sets the box or line the entry is in, never the entry's own text, so Bold given at Jurisdiction: Delaware bolds every Delaware box, whether or not it writes its jurisdiction; but Format, how a text reads, is the entry's own. A box no style gives a look, with nothing at the entry a row of the Key goes by, whose look is exactly that row's, would pass as what the row says it is (an entity with no Entity Type, as a Corporation): it's drawn with a grey dotted outline instead, the page says so at Export ("Pinecrest Capital has no Entity Type"), and a style given it, as ever, decides.

**Settings.** A **setting** is a mark and one of its properties. Its value is the first that applies: entered for that one record in this view (narrowest); for a position, the layout's; the styles'; the default (the widest note, in no view). Entered wins, as everywhere.

**Ink.** A text with no color of its own, the default's or its entry's style's everywhere, which know nothing of the box it's in, is drawn in white on a fill too dark for ink (one on which white reads better than black), where its color wouldn't read there (under 3:1); so a navy fill never leaves dark jurisdictions on navy. A color given the text or its box, for that box or a group, is drawn as given.

**Shown** is a property of every mark. A mark is **drawn** when it is shown and the marks it sits in or joins are drawn. A style showing or hiding by output is a view's filter; a condition worth filtering by is a tag of the chart, computed or entered. What's made on a chart, the chart shows: a box made on a chart view that the view would draw nowhere (its filter hides the kind) is shown there by the same change, a Shown note at its own tree, the narrowest, whether from Insert, a handle dragged, or a transfer or an insertion to one named as it's typed, which a line on the chart asks for. What's added from a list in the panel is left to the view: a page that doesn't show its like doesn't show it. **A record made anywhere but on the chart, related to nothing that page draws, isn't placed on it:** one named new in a list's Add or its table's new row, or imported or pasted, is kept off each chart view that would draw it with no line of its own to what's drawn (a director, on a page with no positions; an entity imported on its own) by the same change, a Shown note at its own tree there, read on the day what's related to it holds, so a stake from a later day is a line; and the toast says so ("Elena isn't on Structure"), with Put on the chart, which is that note the other way.

**Fields.** The texts in a box, or on a line, are its **fields**: the entries of its record that are shown, in their entries' order, then its free text. A field is shown or hidden by a Shown note at the entry's pattern at a **scope**: at the box's object (this box: EIN at Fund III), at its kind (every entity: EIN at Entity), or, for a group of boxes alike by an option or a tag of theirs, at that output with the field's label as the note's param, the field *beside* it (EIN beside Vehicle Type: Main Fund), which reaches the field in each box a record fitting the output is in, as a format given there lands on that box. As for every setting, the narrowest decides: one box's own before a group's; a field beside an output before one at the entry alone, as it is the entry in those boxes only; between groups, the later style. A note at the entry wherever it is (Jurisdiction, at no kind) shows it where there is one, in every box that has it, kept true as more get it: a field's eye or its menu sets it, and as a choice alone wouldn't say what it is, with its name besides (Format Label: "Risk Rating: High"), unless a note says how it reads; a note at a scope names a field of each box there, so where the record has none yet (an EIN not yet typed) the field is **blank**: its entry's name stands in its place, faint, to type into, in a box selected, pointed at or in the scope chosen; elsewhere the box keeps its room empty, as the PDF and the deck do. A blank may be of an entry the chart has at another kind (a Jurisdiction shown in a person's box), which the kind takes as it's typed, as Add gives it. A field's look is given at its entry's pattern, as any text's is; at a group, beside its output, as Shown is, so it reaches that field in each box of the group and nothing else in them (Bold for Jurisdiction beside Jurisdiction: Cayman Islands bolds the jurisdiction of every Cayman box, not its name).

**Free text.** A **Text** note at a scope gives each box or line it reaches one more text, after its fields. Its value is { text, …look }, and an `{Entry}` in its text reads as that entry of the mark's record reads, as a form letter's field does ("Formed {Formation Date}" reads "Formed February 14, 2023"), or as nothing where the record has none. It lands as a format does and the narrowest decides, so a box's own takes the place of its group's, and an empty one gives none.

**Footnotes.** A **Footnote** note at a location, its value { text, field }, its param a name of its own, so a box carries one of each and the narrowest of a name decides: an `{Entry}` in its text reads as a free text's does, for each mark it reaches, and an empty one gives none. A free shape's are in its row (`footnotes`). What carries one has a **marker**, a superscript number after its first line, or after its field, where a field was selected as it was added; and the page has a shape with the role `footnotes`, which comes with the first on the page: one line each, numbered in reading order on that page (down its rows, then across), one number for one text however many carry it, each number a superscript run. So the canvas, the PDF and the deck each draw them as they draw any part of a text, native and editable. The page places it across its margins, just above its footer, the chart keeping clear of it, or, where a chart placed stands there, as far down and left as it's clear of what's drawn, as wide as its words. It takes a look, moved by hand, font, size and columns, as the Key does, and can be hidden in a view, markers and all; what's shown of it prints. It's begun from Insert, a box's, line's or shape's right-click, the panel's Add or Ctrl+Alt+F, Word's key, typed where its line will go, a `{` offering the entries in reach. Its line clicked is selected, Delete taking it away for all that carry it, as a marker clicked in a box selected is; double-clicked, or its row in the panel clicked, it's typed over. Each is one change.

**One change per act.** Showing or hiding a field, by any way the window has (the inspector's Show on chart, its label dragged onto a box, a field dragged off its box, a copy and paste, the box's menu), writes its Shown notes at the pen, with the entries it's in where those are hidden; writing, editing or taking away a free text writes its Text note there. A row dragged onto a box the pen reaches goes to all it reaches, them lit as it's dragged; onto one it doesn't, to that one. A field selected and dragged off its box, to the panel or into empty room, is hidden at the pen. A free text can be begun in place, or in the panel by its Add, a `{` there offering the entries in reach. Each is one change, and Undo takes it back whole.

**Frames.** A format may draw a frame, a box or a bracket around the boxes it lands on, labeled by its caption: Jurisdiction: Cayman Islands draws "Offshore". Outputs of one style captioned alike share a frame, and a relation's frames the box at its lower end. Frames nest in the order of the styles. A box placed among entered ones that's in a frame goes among that frame's boxes, wherever its relatives are, so it stays in its frame and no frame is drawn across another; where boxes put by hand draw one across another all the same, no heading overprints: the later says its own along from the other's.

**Free shapes.** A view's page also holds shapes bound to no record: text, a line or arrow, a picture, a frame. A text may be a formula (`="As of " & TEXT(@AsOf, "mmmm d, yyyy")`). A picture can be cropped: each side's share of the whole cut off, typed in its panel, its frame shrinking round what's left, where it stood, which the PDF and the deck cut as the canvas does (in the deck, PowerPoint's own crop). A free shape is the page's, as a PowerPoint shape is the slide's: it stands where it's put, beside the title as anywhere, and is no part of the chart, which it never moves, nor of what the page holds, which it never grows; only one that would fall off the page is brought back onto it. On another paper it's as large as it printed on its own and keeps its distance from the sheet's nearest corner, so a logo inset on screen stays inset. A text box inserted goes in the page's empty room round the chart nearest the middle of the screen, or, where there's none, in the chart's, with room for a line, clear of every box, line, label and the Key; with something selected, it's about that, and goes in the free room nearest beside it; made longer than that, it's placed again nearest where it was typed, so it never lands on what's drawn. Its place, as every box's, reads in the page's points from the page's top left, and its Height holds what's typed where that holds its text; what it can't hold, by its text or the page, it says ("Height stays 36 pt: its text needs that"). It turns about its middle, by the handle over it or its Rotation typed, canvas, PDF and deck alike, and it flips, across or upside down, its shape mirrored in its frame and its words left to read; a group turns about the group's middle and flips as one, each of its free shapes with it; a box doesn't turn or flip, as its lines run square to it and its shape says what it is, which is said where it's asked, nor do the Key, the title or the date.

**A line is a thing.** A free line is its two ends, a point each, any distance and any angle apart; it has a handle at each end, its Left, Top, Width and Height as any shape has, and its Rotation, which is the way it runs. An end can be **glued**: to a box, or any free shape but a line, a group or the Key. Glued, it says the id of what it's glued to and no more: no record, no meaning, no row of the Key. It's where that faces the line's other end, as that's dragged, tidied or laid out again, and in the deck it's a real connector, glued to the same shape, and follows it there. So a **connector** is a free line glued at either end, called so, and only then. An end dropped on anything gluable glues; dropped in the open, it lets go. A line drawn from a box's dots to another box asks what holds between the two, as ever, with **Just a line, no relation** first among what it offers; from or to a free shape, where nothing could hold, it only glues. A line's Connector (its route) is straight, or elbow, its legs square between its ends. A relation's line has handles too, but it isn't a drawing: its end dropped on another box asks whether it's to go there, as what it would be. At the holder's end that's a transfer, its bar set to that box; at the other, the same relation of another thing, a change pending, ended on the day and made anew from it, with what's entered at it. A box it can't be says so, and nothing's begun.

**Drawn by hand.** Insert's shapes, lines and arrows are drawn: picked, the pointer is a crosshair, a drag draws it to size, a click places it as large as it comes, Enter puts it in free room, and Esc lets it go; a line picked with two things selected that can be glued joins them at once. A text box is typed straight in where the page has room, or drawn so too. What a hand makes too small for its words says so (they're never broken mid-word). Shift keeps a shape's proportions and a line to steps of 15 degrees; Ctrl draws and sizes from the middle; on a turn, Shift snaps to 15 degrees. A picture's proportions are locked till they're unlocked, and it lands in the free room there is, smaller where that's what it takes. Ctrl+drag copies, the copy drawn where it would land. **Ctrl+D duplicates.** A free shape is copied a step down and right, its glue let go. A box is a record, so a copy of it is a new one: of its kind, with what's chosen at it and every look given it alone, none of what's typed at it, named as it's typed ("New entity like Holdco"), empty making nothing; several at once are named for what they're copies of. **Set as default** on a free shape makes its look what the next of its family, shape, line or text box, takes on this chart. A free shape can have a **name** and **alt text** of its own, the deck's Selection Pane name and description; it can be **locked**, so no hand moves, sizes or turns it, and **hidden**, so it's in no scene. **The Selection Pane** (Arrange, Alt+F10, Find by Selection, Layers or Lock) lists all that's drawn on the page in its one order, front first (a free shape keeps its place in it however often it's moved or restyled, by when it was made among those of one Layer; a row dragged takes a Layer between its new neighbours, or the fewer of what's over or under it take one more), each named as it's called: a click selects it however buried, a free shape's double-click names it, a drag reorders, by Layer; each has its Hide, and a free shape its lock; what's hidden stays listed, with Show. A picture has its crop by handles (Crop, on its menu and panel) as well as typed, and Change picture, its frame's width kept. A callout points where its yellow handle puts its tip.

**Words, part by part.** A shape's words are typed as PowerPoint's: Enter begins a paragraph, and Ctrl+Enter, Esc or a click away ends the typing. A free shape's text, and a box's free text, can have **runs**: { from, to, …look }, of its letters as typed; the look any of Font, Size, Bold, Italic, Underline, Strike, Color and Base (up, a superscript; down, a subscript). While it's being typed, what's selected, or the word the caret's in, takes a look from the format bar or its keys (Ctrl+B, Ctrl+I, Ctrl+U, Ctrl+Shift+X, Ctrl+Shift+= and Ctrl+=, Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[), the look given only where it's other than the text's. The scene sets each line's runs as its **spans**; the canvas, the PDF and the deck each draw them and nothing else, the deck as PowerPoint's own runs. A shape's words can be bulleted or numbered, indented, its inner margins each side's, and **Shrink on overflow** sets its words smaller by PowerPoint's steps till they hold, and the deck says PowerPoint's own shrink goes on from there. Too small for its text and not shrinking, a box or shape never breaks a word for a width a hand gave it: it runs over, and Export and the toast say so ("Holdco: its text runs out of its box").

**Annotations stay with what they annotate.** As a chart goes from one layout to another by Tidy or another paper, a free shape laid over boxes, their middles under it, goes round them where they now stand, as far off them as it was; one laid partly over a box goes with that box; a line's end at a box it isn't glued to goes with the box; a glued one follows anyway. What's over nothing stays where a free shape stays, and it's said ("the logo stays where it was: it's on no box"). This is worked out from the two scenes alone (Scene.annotated), written by the same change, and shown in their previews.

**Layers.** What's drawn on a page is in one order: by Layer, and within a layer frames under lines under boxes under free shapes, the canvas, the PDF and the deck alike. So a rectangle sent to back goes behind boxes and lines, a shaded band behind the offshore entities, and it's picked and dragged by any part of it that shows. Send to back and Bring to front go past everything; Forward and Backward past the next it overlaps. With nothing to go past, it's said ("Already at the back") and nothing's written; going past what it doesn't overlap, that's said too.

**The legend** is a shape bound to the view's styles. Its rows are, for each style and each output from which a drawn mark takes a format, a swatch drawn in that format and its **caption** (the output, unless a Caption note says otherwise), outputs alike in format and caption sharing a row: a Caption note calls only the output it's at, and outputs alike with none are called by what they are ("AIV and Feeder Fund"). A row's outputs are in their option list's order, and the rows in the view's order of its styles, those of a style that is its one row (Names) after the rest, or in the Key's own order once its rows have been dragged, a row new to it after those. Styles lists the same rows, of every output, and the scope those that reach the selection. It is moved, sized, formatted and edited like any shape, by its title or edge: a press on a row drags that row among the rest, on the page as in its panel, where each has a grip. In its panel a row's box shows or hides it where it stands, so no row moves under the pointer; its caption double-clicked is typed over, the Key's own, in its row (its `captions`), which any line saying the relation says too and a frame's heading doesn't; and its right-click offers Rename, Hide and Reset. A line's swatch shows its arrowheads, but Auto's, which go by which way a line runs. It holds its place as the chart changes, and Tidy places it afresh; it gives way to what the layout places and to what a hand puts on it, never to what a hand puts or bends near it. Its menu and panel put it in a **corner** by name, within the margins (Top left, under the title where it would run over it; Bottom right), its `corner`, holding there however large it grows, on any paper, so what's under it is said in Export and Tidy sets the chart clear of it: to its side as far as the margin lets, then smaller, but never smaller than leaves its gaps a chart's; past that the Key goes over it, and Export says so.

**Changes.** A chart view may mark what's changed, by a Changes note at the view: since a date it names, or since the page before it, the chart view before it in tab order, as of the day that keeps, or today where it keeps none. Asked in its panel or Find, that's a look, in Viewing too: the note is this window's, in no log, till Keep makes it the page's, as Next step's already is; so comparing two dates is reading, and writes no one's name. It's compared with the view as of that day, by what the view draws, mark by mark, so nothing's marked that can't be seen and what's marked can't go stale: a box or line drawn now and not then is **New**; drawn both days, with a text that reads otherwise or is new or gone, or another look (a style that follows a fact), **Changed**; drawn then and not now, **Gone**. On a day not after the day compared with, nothing is. These are the options of one entry, Changes, which any record can have, its location left open as Evidenced's is; the comparison gives one at each mark's record and nothing enters them. So its style is a style like any other: the outputs Changes: New, Changed and Gone, each with its formats, a Styles row and a Key row (New and Changed look and are called alike, "New or changed", so they're one; a Caption or a look given either tells them apart), aimed, restyled, ordered, copied and carried by a look as any is, and after the view's other styles, so it decides, but before a tag's, so what's failed stays red. It begins in a look of no colour the chart uses to say anything else, not the Key's red: pale yellow with a blue outline and dark blue text, a line blue; what's gone unfilled, grey, dashed and struck. Taken off, the look goes with it unless it's been changed. **The scene takes the comparison:** what's gone is brought back into what's drawn, with its entries, its look as of that day and its lines, so it stands where it stood by the one set of places, under what's still there; and a text that reads otherwise has what it read before it, struck, in what's gone's look, beside it on a line's label ("100% 85%"), over it in a box. So the canvas, the PDF and the deck draw it and nothing else, and all agree, a deck's lines named as of their own page's day, what's gone among them. What's gone is no record of the day shown: selected, its panel says when it ended, and it can be moved or hidden but not typed in or deleted.

**The layout** works out every position not entered, together: drawn boxes and their sizes, frames as clusters, the lines that rank boxes (a line's Above names the edge drawn on top, as Owner), and the routes of lines. **A view's places are one set:** a box has one place, on every day, whichever day was shown when it was entered, so a page as of any day is that day's boxes in those places, with what has none placed beside them; an edit made as of one day moves nothing on another, and opening the page again moves nothing at all. **A plan has one look and one set of places:** views made by Next step from one another (see The view) are the pages of one **plan**, by a Plan note at each, one chart on several days. What a change puts on one of them, a place, a bend, a scale, a paper or a look, of one box, a Styles row, All text or a whole look given (Match look, a house style), the same change puts on them all, said once, so a box moved or restyled on one page is so on every page and none differs between them. Each keeps its own name, its day, what it shows and hides, and a box's free text. A page leaves the plan by its panel's Look and places, both its own from then on; Duplicate makes a view of no plan. **Tidy** lays the boxes drawn on the day shown out afresh and enters what it lays out, by one change: it reads each box only for where it stood across, its order among what's beside it, which the layout keeps, so what's beside each other stands in the order it stood in, or, where nothing has stood, the order it was made in where no crossing is saved by leaving that, never reversed, and the chart is set across the middle of the page; a place of a box not drawn that day goes, and that box is placed again beside them on the days it's drawn. **What's across is set by the stems**, as a structure chart is read down them: a box and what its one line joins it to stand in one column, so the line drops straight (an investor over its fund, as wide as the fund is; a blocker under its fund); first a stem that's each end's only line of the main relation, then a parent's to the one of its only-owned children nearest their middle, then a box's one line to what it joins, each only where the tiers' boxes still have the room they need. A stem that passes a tier is a thing no box in that tier may be put across: each goes on the side where its own lines cross fewest of them, a trunk counted once. Boxes owned by the same two or more stand together, a gap apart, in the order they had. Everything else stands as near its relatives as the columns leave it, and the chart then uses the width its scale leaves it, every gap in it alike, up to 54 pt, and a little of the height, each row up to 15 pt further from the next, so its lanes have room where it draws it no smaller. On a page nobody has arranged, no box placed by a hand and Tidy not yet used, what's across is only the order the boxes were made in, of no one's choosing, and its first Tidy is free of it; after that Tidy keeps the order they stand in. So Tidy loses no column, drops nothing out of what belongs together, and runs no rule across a stem it could go round; the sample's Structure is held to it, crossings, bends, straight lines, width used and the lines its names take, no worse than as it's composed, on every page and on slide and Letter (scene-test). With two or more boxes selected, or a box's own Reset, it does so with theirs alone, and they go beside their relatives, around what stands, the Key among it, which stays where it is; its name and its preview say which ("Tidy 2 boxes"); one box selected is only what was last worked on, and it's Tidy of the page. **Tidy for every date**, beside it in Arrange and in Find, lays out as one chart every box the view draws on any day, of the records of every day that has a different set of them, and enters those places, so a step plan is the same chart on two days with only what changed moving: but a line ranks nothing there that skips what's between its ends on no day it holds with it (Fund III's to the Blocker, before Holdco came between them), and on those days it runs straight through its empty place. Its preview says it's that. **A change keeps what it doesn't touch:** made while a chart is shown, it enters, in that view, the place of every box drawn there that the layout placed, where it's drawn (a kept place, which History doesn't say), so a box's place is laid out once and then stands: in a chart placed, as an entered place reads, where the layout put it among them before the room their lanes take moved it with them, so it's drawn there again, and an edit that places nothing moves no box and re-routes no line. A box placed among entered ones goes beside its relatives, on no line it isn't on: in the row of those that share them by its own relation (a new owner among the owners of what it owns: a buyer into Halvard among Halvard's owners, an investor imported among its fund's), nearest their middle, where that row has room no more than a box off them; or else that row's boxes slide apart by the least that's needed, keeping their order, for it to stand beside them, in the order the new ones were made, those boxes' lines moving with them and nothing else moving, unless it was dropped by hand or tidied around what stands, which moves nothing, nearest where it was dropped, at the row's end where nothing nearer is clear, and only where the row is full, on a page that stays as it is, in a row of its own over or under them; where none shares them by its relation, in the row of those that share them by another (its fund's general partner's) where it has room near them, or in a row of its own over or under them, or between rows; one over triangles by a line that says what it is (a general partner's) beside them, its near side over their slope. A box made on the chart before it's related is placed loosely, where it was made; once a line that ranks it joins it, it goes so from where it was made, so a box dropped stays near where it was dropped, on that side of its relatives (made within its width of their middle, square over or under them), and on the right side of them, wherever it was dropped: an owner over what it owns, by as much as its line needs to come down, its line into the apex, never level with it or under it, and nothing else moving; but of loose boxes joined only to each other, the first made stands. So a box added goes beside its relatives, and nothing else moves. **The pen** is the scope, made visible and movable. There's one answer to what a change reaches: a **location** in the schema, which is a pattern, the notes being written at patterns already. What's selected says what (the box, or a field in it); the pen says for whom. The locations of a page are: its **title**, this box or line alone, its own tree; the **kind** in its eyebrow, every one of the kind, or on a line's page its **relation**; a **choice or tag** row, at any depth, every box with that answer, and where the Styles row that answer's in is wider ("Funds": Main Fund, Parallel Fund…), that first, a second click going to the one answer; a **free value** row, every box that has that entry, a look landing on the box as an option's does, a text property beside each of its fields; a **list** of relatives, its heading every box with that list and a row of it every box with that relative in it, by each box's own tree, as what a box is a side of is no pattern of it; and on a line's page, a **side**, every line with that record at it. A count clicked, in the bar or in Find, opens **what's open**: one list under the view, over the foot of a chart and under a table's rows, with a tab for each count and All, which stays as its rows are clicked, so five errors are five clicks, the chart scrolled by as little as brings what's chosen out from under it. It closes by its ×, Esc or its count again. Each row says what it is, whose, its date or figure ("Ownership Adds Up · Fund III", 101.6%, not 100%; "Annual Tax Due · AIV-1, tax year 2025", Jun 1, 2026), who's responsible and why, in the words the formula bar has; found as they're typed; choosing one goes there. What's dated is kept to the next 30, 60 or 90 days beside what's overdue, by one choice. **Copy** and **Excel** take what's listed, by its kind, with who and why, each kind marked as it's marked here, the file's first line saying the day and the counts. Under Blank, what's required comes first; then, apart and folded elsewhere, blank, not required, by what's blank ("EIN (7)"), and not ours, each row saying what would change that (see Blank by choice). **The keys.** Everything the pointer does has a key a person can find, and the keys are always somewhere that can be seen. The window's **panes** are stops of F6 and Shift+F6, top down as they're drawn: the title bar, the format bar, the formula bar, a table's toolbar, the view, the bar a change pending has, a message with something to do (Show, Undo), which stays while the keys are on it, the inspector, the tabs; a pane with nothing to take the keys is no stop. **The keys are never nowhere:** what had them can go (a button a filter's clearing took away, a layer closed, the panel gone on to something else), and then they go back to the pane they were last in, or else the view, at once where the keys came last, and at the latest with the next key. A grid takes that key, as what's typed into a sheet; anywhere else it's spent finding them, so a key meant for something gone never rewrites what's here. Where the pointer came last, none of this shows; where the keys did, a menu opens on its ticked item or its first, a tooltip shows at once on what they land on, an icon saying its name, and the box they're on on a chart has a ring as plain as any field's. In a table the cell they're on has the focus's ring; with them elsewhere, it's where they were, dashed in grey; with no cell (what's selected filtered out), the grid has the ring. **A picker** (a palette's swatches, the shapes, the calendar's days, Insert, a shadow's direction, a row of choices, Export's pages) is one stop for Tab, whichever choice it was last on, and the arrows go to the nearest choice that way as it's drawn, each previewed and saying its name as it's reached, Enter taking it; a color it already has, taken again, is no change, so a look stays its style's. A menu's row of choices (Editing and Viewing, Light and Dark) is on the arrows' path, and its own arrows go across it. **A list a click opens** (History's entries, a record's relatives, what a selection of many lists) is one stop for Tab, Up and Down going through its rows, Home and End to its ends, what's in a row a stop only while the row is the list's; Enter opens it, and Shift+F10 or the context-menu key, anywhere the keys are, is a right-click there: a box's menu, a cell's, a row's, an entry's. An entry of History gives so what's done with it (Show the chart as it stood, What it holds, Undo this change, Change…, Say why…), Undo without going to the moment first; History opened has the keys on its first entry, and closed gives them back to what had them. **Why** is F1, at what the keys are at where a formula gives it or a rule covers it (a row, a cell) or else what's selected, the keys going into the reason, and back out with Esc, Alt+← and Alt+→ reading on with it open; with no why to give, F1 is the list of keys. A word for your own reason is **Say why…**, on an entry, a rule and a change's bar, so "Why" alone is never a blank box to type in. **Going box to box:** Tab keeps its reading order; Ctrl+Alt+arrow goes to the box that way, of those whose middle is past this one's edge, what stands in line before what's off to the side, the nearest first, and writes nothing, where an arrow alone nudges; Esc lets go of a box and not the place, so the next goes on from there. Relate… by the keys is Tab or Ctrl+Alt+arrow to the other box, drawn to as a pointer draws, and Enter or Space, which ask the two's relations as a drop does, the keys going to the first of them; a name typed makes a new box and asks the same, as a drop in empty room does; Esc makes nothing, and the box it began from is selected again. **Esc** backs out a step, always: a layer closes and the keys go back to what opened it (a submenu's to the menu it opened from, which goes on where it was); from any pane (a toolbar, the tabs, the panel), to the view, at what's selected, which stays so; and from the panel with nothing selected, it puts the panel away. **The list of keys** opens by ?, F1, Ctrl+/ and from the chart menu, in Find's field, found by what a key does or by the keys themselves; every key is on it, under where it works, each part saying its own; and a menu item says its key. Find has by name what the toolbars have (Clear filters, Delete this row…, Zoom in, Show today), and each list a record could have, one with nothing on it yet too (Add owner…), and "today" is a day, typed where a day is. In a table Ctrl+Minus deletes the rows of the cells selected, Shift+F10 is the row's menu, Alt+Down on a list's cell opens its list, and a copy says how many cells it took. Zoom goes through its levels about what's selected, or else the middle of what's on screen. A deletion or an undo begun by the keys takes them to its bar, on its day, what its day is, or else its verb, so what Enter would do is never done with nothing lit.

**Launch.** The dappled wall shows once per opened tab and dims into the page. On the very first launch a tour follows, each step done by doing what it teaches, and said by what's being used, the pointer or the keys: select a box (Tab to one); type in its name (Enter on one); choose all like it (Ctrl+Shift+A); drag a dot to another box (Shift+F10, Relate…, Tab to one, Enter); point at the two boxes' panel the drag made (F6 to it); select an italic value (Tab to one); switch views (Ctrl+Shift+PgDn). ## Part 3. The look and the words

**Words.** No subtitles, descriptions, hints or tips. An empty state is a few words and its one action. A tooltip names an icon-only control and its key. A placeholder is "Find", or "New" and what it makes ("New option", "New relation", "New entity", "New column"), never an example or an instruction. A toast is a verb, an object and Undo; one for a change that takes something out of sight offers where it went ("Show Jan 15, 2027"), and stays till that's taken or it's dismissed. It never covers what's being worked on, the field typed in, the cell or box selected, or the pointer: it stands at the foot of the view, or else at its foot's left or right or at its top, the first that's clear, placed as it comes and as the selection or the keys move, never as the pointer does; and for a moment in each place it takes no click, so one meant for what was there goes there. Nothing on screen says demo; the workbook a first launch opens says it's the sample, in the chart menu's list of workbooks, and Start blank says that's where it's kept. Teaching belongs to the tour. The model's terms (specification, pattern, note, candidate) never appear. Where a relation is, it's named by its sides, two as their line joins them (Fund III → Fund III Blocker), three or more as its sentence says them (Margaret A. Chen for Fund III, via GP III), the same everywhere.

**Computed** is italic, and italic means nothing else; a computed tag's box is outlined, and a computed line in the default style is dashed: on screen only, like Excel's trace arrows. An entered value that says other than its formula keeps a small amber corner; one that agrees, a paid tax year say, overrides nothing and has none. A tag its rule hasn't tested reads n/a, grey; one it can't say for want of a value reads Can't say, amber, and sorts and filters as its own value. In a table each says so on hover. Errors show in place.

**Type.** IBM Plex Sans throughout, formulas included, with their functions in small capitals; four sizes; weights 400, 500, 600. The chart's text is the document's: Arial by default, which PowerPoint and the PDF draw alike, or any of forty fonts from Office, Windows and the Mac, each drawn where it isn't installed by the fonts of its metrics (Calibri by Carlito), or any font typed. PowerPoint names the font itself. The PDF embeds the face the canvas draws, this computer's, with only the letters used, Arial as much as any, once the browser lets its fonts be read (it asks once), so a viewer or a print shop sets what was laid out; one that can't be read or embedded, or that this computer hasn't, is in the standard font of its class, never silently: saving says so by the font's name, with what to do, and the toast stays a while ("Georgia printed as Times: this browser hasn't let it read your fonts: allow fonts for this page in the address bar, then export again"; "Arial printed as Helvetica: …"). A line its font would set wider than the scene is condensed to the scene's width, so no text leaves its box. Icons are Lucide's, familiar ones, few, with menus in words.

**The page's own look.** What a chart prints in, where nothing says otherwise, is ink and a slate rule, not black: names in ink (#1c2733), outlines half a point in the rule's slate (#3a4653), lines three quarters. The sample, and so every chart begun from it (its look being the house look), goes further, as a good annual report's page does: its title Georgia, plain, in navy (#1f3a5f), and its date quiet; names plain, so a page's subject can lead, in navy with its name in bold (the main fund on the fund's pages, the BDC on its affiliates'), which its title already names, so it's no row of the Key; other funds a pale slate (#dde4ec), investors sand (#efe9dc), portfolio companies stone (#f4f3ef), none an Office tint; and no box round the Key. A look a user has given stays as given.

**Color.** Warm paper and ink, hairlines rather than cards or shadows, and one slate-navy accent for the primary action, the selection and focus. Green attested, or good news; red what's wrong now: a check failed, a rule broken, a typed no a Check note says to heed (Good Standing: No), overdue, a change that adds an error; and the redline's ink for what would go, as a comparison's is, in a struck word. An error a change takes away is good news, and green. Amber what wants doing, and nothing's wrong yet: a blank that's required, due soon, a value typed over a rule, a rule that can't say for want of a value. A fact is one colour everywhere it's read: chart, panel, table and status bar. A guide as a box is dragged is its own blue, the page's; a grip that reshapes is white in the accent. A typed no a plain grey X. Dark is tuned on its own; the chart's page stays white, and the swatches drawn on it in the panel dimmed whole, so a plate isn't the brightest thing there. Text is 4.5:1 or more against what it sits on, in both themes; a field's edge and the focus's ring 3:1 or more; what can't be pressed now is quieter than any text, and still 3:1, never faded till it can't be told from nothing. A text color's bar stands on a plate that shows it in dark.

**States.** One language, for every control in the window:

| State | Looks | Where it isn't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At rest | ink, no fill; a field or a dropdown a 1 px edge (`--edge`), one outline for all of them | |
| Hover | the hover wash over what it has | what's disabled |
| Pressed down | the press wash | a primary's darkens |
| On (a toggle, a segment, a pick) | the selection's tint and the accent's ink: Bold, the panel's button, This box, Landscape, the day shown's chip | a day in the calendar, filled in the accent, as the one thing it's asked for |
| Open (its menu is showing) | the press wash | |
| Focus | a 2 px ring, for the keys alone; what a mouse gave focus to has none, however it got it, but a field being typed in. The window goes by the hand last used: the keys by what takes them somewhere (Tab, the arrows off a chart, F6, Find, a menu's key), not Enter or Esc, what's typed, an arrow nudging on a chart or a chord that acts where it stands | |
| Disabled | faint, and it says why, pointed at | |

A segmented control is one thing, its parts sharing an edge, the one that's on as above. A dialog ends in Cancel and a verb, or Done; never OK. A dropdown is one thing, the window's own list, in a dialog too (`Pick`), never the browser's. A yes-or-no's box says in words, pointed at, which of its states it's in: yes, typed (filled); yes, worked out (outlined); no, typed (a grey or a red X); no, worked out; nobody has said (empty); n/a, in words, its rule doesn't apply; Can't say, in words and in amber, its rule wants a value. Typed over a rule, it keeps its amber corner. So they need no legend.

**Motion.** Object constancy: as the chart drawn changes, by Tidy, Undo, a view switched, a paper, a day shown or a change made, what's drawn before and after **travels** from where it was on screen to where it is, 200 ms, eased, its size with it, its lines from their old route to their new, point for point, and the sheet with them; what's new comes up and what's gone goes down. So the eye keeps hold of each box. The cells are where they end from the first, so nothing reads or writes a place that isn't so, and a hand on the chart lands it at once. A look pointed at in the format bar changes as fast as the pointer moves, and is simply there; Tidy's preview, which is rested on, travels. Never with reduced motion, nor where it would cost a frame: past 360 cells on screen it's skipped, and a window that twice misses a frame lands where it's going, and skips what's as large from there on. Work done ahead waits till it's landed. A box's snap shows while it's dragged, the row it would join or the grid, so letting go moves nothing. A new box's placeholder is the size of the middle box of its kind, and it goes from there to the box it makes. **Play**, on the date's ruler, runs the chart through the days its structure changes, one each half a second or as long as it takes to draw, and comes back to the day it began on; any hand on the ruler, or Esc, stops it.

**Crisp.** A stroke on the canvas is a whole number of the screen's pixels, by the zoom; a line square to the screen is drawn without anti-aliasing, so it falls on them, and a box's edges are set on them, by a hair's move and stretch of its outline at this zoom and scroll. So strokes of one weight render alike at any zoom. It's the screen's alone: nothing's place changes, and the PDF and the deck print the weight as it is. Export's previews are the file's own vectors.

**A selection is quiet.** At rest, one outline by the box's own shape. Its grips stand up as the pointer nears them, and its dots stay small till the pointer's coming to one; neither sits on a line's label. An entry's name shows on hover, once the pointer has moved, never on the press that selected it.

**The ruler** (the date's) lies over a chart's foot, so the chart doesn't move as it comes and goes, and under a table's last row, and above a pending change's bar, which keeps its place at the foot, so what's on it stays under the hand: the years a scale along its bottom, the chart's days rising from a hairline, tall and dark where something comes to be or ends, middling where a relation moves, short and faint where a date is only noted, and the day shown its hand. It stays while a hand is on it, whatever the day, and a press on it puts away the popover that opened it, to see the chart. The date's chip is only as wide as what it says, in room kept for its longest, at the right.

**The formula bar** is the view's, not the window's: over a table, in its own row, so what it opens to pushes the rows down; over a chart, on the chart's top edge, so nothing moves as it comes and goes, and with nothing selected and nothing pending it gives the room back. It closes what it's opened to as another tab is shown. A dialog's head and foot stay whatever the window, only what's between them scrolling, and with no pages to preview it's as tall as what it says. On a small window Find gives way before the chart's name; the inspector is a quarter of the window, 280 to 360 px; a toast keeps clear of what's drawn as well as what's being worked on, where it can.

**The mark** is a tree grown by one rule, each limb ending in two, with its roots the same rule reflected below.
